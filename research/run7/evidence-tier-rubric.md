# Evidence-strength rubric — A00e proposal

Draft for Kevin's review, 2026-10-28. No tier is approved or assigned in the app.
The examples show how to use the criteria. They are not final ratings of whole records.

Rate support for one stated claim, within its scope. Do not rate a faith, tradition, person, or whole book.
First name the claim and the evidence that could test it. Separate printed wording from claims about past events.
For example, a clear difference between printed numbers does not establish which number came first.

Keep these tiers separate from the approved labels. A label describes a finding; a tier describes its support.
Any label may have limited support. An open finding may have strong evidence that a key issue remains unresolved.
Instruction in context describes a text's role. Do not use it as proof that an instruction applies today.

## Direct support

Definition: The cited evidence directly supports the stated claim within clear limits.

Criteria: The claim matches the quoted text or a repeatable calculation. The compared items and scope are clear.
No unresolved field conflict or unstated link is needed for that claim. Any broader conclusion needs its own review.
For science or history, check that the cited study tests the stated claim with a suitable method.

- `numbers-levite-total-discrepancy`: The listed counts total 22,300; the stated total is 22,000. This supports a count difference, not an original reading.
- `jews-slew-seventy-five-thousand-or-fifteen-thousand-greek`: The cited texts print different totals. This supports that wording comparison, not a count of deaths in history.

## Support with limits

Definition: The evidence supports a narrower claim, while a stated limit prevents a broader conclusion.

Criteria: State the supported part and the remaining limit separately. Keep each condition beside the claim it limits.
The evidence must support the narrow claim without choosing an unresolved identity, date, or reading.
Missing evidence alone does not establish that an event did not happen.

- `hezekiah-hearkened-to-the-envoys-or-was-glad-of-them`: Listening and being glad can both occur. The different verbs do not establish incompatible events.
- `hiram-oil-twenty-measures-or-twenty-thousand-baths`: The accounts name different recipients. Without a unit conversion, their quantities do not establish a shortfall.

## Mixed support

Definition: Relevant parts of the record support different readings, and the record does not resolve their weight.

Criteria: Name the competing claims and the fields that support each. Do not choose one by counting sources.
Hold any label affected by conflicting fields. A tier must never serve as a way around that hold.
These examples show review problems; neither receives a tier or a new label here.

- `three-days-and-three-nights-or-the-third-day-matthew-12-40-matthew-16-21`: Reasoning gives about 72 hours; scope leaves day counting open. Resolve that mismatch before choosing a reading.
- `if-one-man-sin-against-another-the-judge-or-they-shall-pray-1-samuel-2-25`: Reasoning calls the judge human; scope leaves the identity open. That claim needs a field review.

## Too little support

Definition: The record lacks evidence needed to decide the stated claim.

Criteria: Name the missing link or test. Do not treat an untested link as evidence against either reading.
Use this tier when the claim requires a fact the supplied evidence does not establish.
Distinguish a missing link from evidence that supports competing readings.

- `history-theudas-revolt-order`: The date conflict depends on both accounts naming the same man. The record does not establish that shared identity.
- `early-vessels`: A count conflict depends on a complete list. The record leaves open whether the list includes all items.

## Review steps

1. Read the full summary, reasoning, scope, and cited evidence. Keep unverified supplemental prose marked as such.
2. State the narrow claim in plain words. Check whether the evidence addresses that exact claim.
3. Check the method, date, identity, edition, and comparison as relevant. Explain why any unresolved link matters.
4. Record the strongest supported reading and the strongest stated alternative. Preserve the limits of both.
5. Propose a tier only after checking those points. If fields conflict, retain the meaning hold first.

Never infer strength from status, an approved label, source count, artwork, or publication state.
A source list does not show that its sources support a claim. An imagined image supplies no evidence.
Quoted editions show what those editions print. They do not establish agreement across all ancient copies.
Do not assign numerical probabilities or imply a scholarly consensus unless the supplied evidence supports it.
