# Psalms 107–134 — notes (2026-10-13)

15 records, all tagged T (witness: 1 “Hebrew and Septuagint”, 14 “Septuagint”). No V and no untagged conflict. All are drafts until Codex accepts them; every record carries `sides`.

**Why this is 107–134 and not 107–150.** The plan named Psalms 107–150 (44 chapters), which is over the 30-chapter limit for a part. The part was cut at Psalm 134, the end of the songs of degrees. Psalms 135–150 is the next unit (see the README).

## Verse numbers

Brenton’s Greek psalm = KJV psalm − 1 for KJV 107–113 (KJV 107 = `grcbrent/PSA106.htm`). Brenton prints KJV 114 and 115 together as its Psalm 113 (26 verses: KJV 114:1–8 = 113:1–8, KJV 115:1–18 = 113:9–26). KJV 116 is its Psalms 114 (KJV 116:1–9) and 115 (KJV 116:10–19; this page prints a verse 4a and no verse 5, so KJV 116:14 is printed as 115:4a). From KJV 117 the Greek number is one lower to KJV 134 (Greek 133). The Hebrew (WLC) verse number equals the KJV number in every psalm of this part except 108 (one ahead: the title is verse 1). Brenton prints no lettered verses here except 115:4a. Counts of KJV, WLC and Brenton were tabulated for all 28 chapters before quoting.

Records 7–15 follow the T test: a plainly different word, stated as what each text prints, with no lexicon claim and no claim about which is original. In records 2–15 the Hebrew edition agrees with the KJV’s sense and the Septuagint is the differing text; none is described as a mistranslation of the Hebrew.

## What was compared

All 28 chapters (KJV 107–134) were set verse by verse against Brenton’s English of the matching Greek verse by a script (word overlap, then numbers and proper names). Every verse flagged by either test was read in the KJV, the Hebrew and the Greek. The chapters were not read in full in the Hebrew; a verse that the scripts did not flag was not examined in Hebrew or Greek. Psalm 119 (176 verses) was handled the same way, and its repeated word-lists (statutes, precepts, ordinances, testimonies, oracles) were treated as wording and not issued.

## Considered and not issued

- 108:7–9 (Shechem, Succoth, “Judah is my lawgiver” against “Judas is my king”): repeats Psalm 60:6–8, where `judah-is-my-lawgiver-or-judas-is-my-king-greek` is already published. Not re-issued.
- 108:9 “washpot” and 120:4 “juniper” against “of the desert”: a lexicon would be needed for the Hebrew side; the Greek differs but the record would add little.
- 109:6 “Satan” against “the devil”: both name the same figure; the difference is of spelling and of word, and 109:6 is context in a published Q. Left out.
- 109:4 (“For my love”), 109:10 (“seek their bread … out of their desolate places” against “cast out of their habitations”), 109:24 (“faileth of fatness”), 116:11 (“in my haste” against “in mine amazement”): kept out to keep the batch small; the Greek differs and quotes are sourced for 109:10 and 116:11.
- 116:10 with 2 Corinthians 4:13: the Greek psalm and the New Testament agree against the KJV’s “therefore”; the Hebrew conjunction has a range of senses that covers it. 109:8 with Acts 1:20 (“office” against “bishoprick”): the same Greek noun, differently worded in English; wording only. 110:1 with Matthew 22:44 and parallels: the English differs by “at” and “on” and “thy footstool”; no difference of meaning. 118:22 with Matthew 21:42: no difference.
- 131:2 (“Surely I have behaved”): the Greek reads as an oath form that the Hebrew words can also carry; no plain difference.
- 107:27 (“at their wit’s end”), 107:12, 107:38–39, 115:18 (“we, the living”: an addition that states only what the contrast with “the dead” implies): left out.
- The Greek of 116:14 stands at 115:4a (a repeat of verse 9); no comparison made.
- Chronicles parallels: Psalm 105:1–15, 106:1 and 106:47–48 are not in this part; Psalm 107:1 (“O give thanks”) against 1 Chronicles 16:34 was not issued, because 1 Chronicles was not compared there and the conflict belongs to the earlier book.
- No V: nothing in Psalms 107–134 describes a breach of a law by someone under it. No untagged conflict: verses were not set against other books beyond the three Q contexts.
