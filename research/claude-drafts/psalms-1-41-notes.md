# Psalms 1–41 (Book I) — notes

2026-10-13. First of five parts of Psalms (1–41, 42–72, 73–89, 90–106, 107–150). Thirteen records in `psalms-1-41-records.json`: 13 G, no H, no V, no untagged. All are drafts until Codex accepts them. Every quotation was asserted by script against the packaged KJV or the fetched page; every side was validated with `scripts/verse-roles.mjs`; all 63 source addresses answered.

## Numbering — read this first

- **Brenton’s Greek prints KJV Psalms 9 and 10 as one psalm** (its Psalm 9, 39 verses). So KJV Psalms 1–8 are Greek 1–8; KJV 9:v is Greek 9:(v+1); KJV 10:v is Greek 9:(v+21); KJV Psalms 11–41 are Greek 10–40. The Greek address is built from the Greek number: KJV Psalm 40 is `grcbrent/PSA039.htm`. Each record says “numbered …” at every Greek quotation, and the source label says “(Greek numbering)”.
- **Titles.** The packaged KJV does not hold the psalm titles. The Hebrew (WLC) and Brenton’s Greek count a title as verse 1 in many psalms and then run one ahead of the KJV. In this part WLC runs one ahead in Psalms 3–9, 12, 18–22, 30, 31, 34, 36, 38–41, and the Greek does the same in the matching psalms; elsewhere the title, if any, is printed inside verse 1. Psalm 13 has the same count in all three but divides its last verses differently; it is not quoted.
- `refs`, `appliesTo` and `sides` take KJV numbers.

## Records — why each qualifies

1. `i-have-set-my-king-upon-zion-or-i-have-been-made-king-by-him-greek` (2:6) — G: the Greek gives the sentence to the king (“by him”, “his holy mountain”); the KJV gives it to the one who sets the king.
2. `break-them-with-a-rod-of-iron-or-rule-them-greek-and-revelation` (2:9) — G: the Greek psalm has the phrase the KJV renders “rule them with a rod of iron” at Revelation 2:27; the KJV psalm has “break”.
3. `kiss-the-son-or-lay-hold-of-instruction-greek` (2:12) — G: no son and no kiss in the Greek verse; the Lord is the one who may be angry.
4. `stand-in-awe-and-sin-not-or-be-ye-angry-and-sin-not-greek-and-ephesians` (4:4) — G: the Greek psalm has the four words of Ephesians 4:26.
5. `god-is-angry-with-the-wicked-every-day-or-not-bringing-wrath-every-day-greek` (7:11) — G: the Greek has a negative; opposite statements about daily anger.
6. `out-of-the-mouth-of-babes-ordained-strength-or-perfected-praise-greek-and-matthew` (8:2) — G: “strength” in the KJV psalm, “praise” in the Greek psalm and in Matthew 21:16.
7. `put-them-in-fear-or-appoint-a-lawgiver-over-them-greek` (9:20) — G: fear in the KJV, a lawgiver in the Greek.
8. `thou-shalt-keep-them-or-keep-us-greek` (12:7) — G: “them” in the KJV, “us” twice in the Greek.
9. `there-is-none-that-doeth-good-greek-adds-their-throat-is-an-open-sepulchre` (14:3) — G: six lines in the Greek verse that the KJV psalm lacks, standing in Romans 3:13–18. The KJV has like lines only in other psalms and in Isaiah, not in the same passage, so the additions rule keeps it.
10. `their-line-is-gone-out-or-their-sound-greek-and-romans` (19:4) — G: “line” in the KJV psalm, “sound” in the Greek psalm and in Romans 10:18.
11. `my-god-my-god-why-hast-thou-forsaken-me-greek-adds-attend-to-me` (22:1) — G: a plea the KJV lacks, and “my transgressions” for “my roaring”.
12. `give-unto-the-lord-o-ye-mighty-or-ye-sons-of-god-bring-young-rams-greek` (29:1) — G: “sons of God” for “ye mighty”, and an added clause about rams.
13. `mine-ears-hast-thou-opened-or-a-body-hast-thou-prepared-me-greek-and-hebrews` (40:6) — G: “ears” in the KJV psalm, “a body” in the Greek psalm and in Hebrews 10:5.

## Points for Codex

- Five records carry a side B on a New Testament verse (Revelation 2:27, Ephesians 4:26, Matthew 21:16, Romans 10:18, Hebrews 10:5). In each the KJV’s own two verses word the same sentence differently, and the G tag sits on the psalm verse only. If a side B reads as a claim of contradiction, it can be dropped; the scopes say the two wordings need not exclude each other.
- The 14:3 record has side A only, following the correction made to the Job concubines record.
- The 22:1 record has two side-A entries on one verse.
- The closing limits of each record now name only the editions it quotes (the lesson from the Job 22–42 Q batch).
- In no record is H tagged. Where the KJV follows the Hebrew and the Greek differs, the scope says the record is not tagged for the Hebrew.
- Brenton’s page for Greek Psalm 28 prints “τῷ Κρίῳ” before “υἱοὺς κριῶν”; the word is left out of the quotation and the scope says so.
- At Matthew 27:46 the Textus Receptus text served has “τι με” where Tischendorf has “ἱνατί με”; both are quoted.

## Considered and not issued

- **22:16, “they pierced my hands and my feet”** — the Leningrad text prints a word with the letters of “like a lion”; Brenton’s Greek has a verb and Brenton’s English has “pierced”, so there is no G, and H would need a dictionary source to show a difference of meaning. Left out and logged in `owner-questions.md`.
- 8:5, “a little lower than the angels” — the Hebrew has the word the KJV usually renders “God”; the Greek agrees with the KJV, so no G; H would rest on how the KJV renders the word elsewhere. Left out, same rule; covered by the same log entry.
- 16:10 — the Greek agrees with the KJV.
- Psalm 18 beside 2 Samuel 22, and the title of Psalm 34 beside 1 Samuel 21 — the other book is finished; the titles are not in the packaged KJV.
- Psalm 14 beside Psalm 53 — two forms of one psalm in verse; no statement of fact conflicts.
- Verses of a psalm set against another book (5:5, 30:5, 37:25) — figures of speech in verse; not proposed.
- No V: the psalms of this part tell no act under the law.
