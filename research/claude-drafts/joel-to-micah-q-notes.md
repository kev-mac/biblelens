## Records

1. `the-northern-army-who-or-what-is-the-northern-army-joel-2-20` — Joel 2:20 promises to remove “the northern army”; 1:6 describes a nation come up on the land, 2:25 calls the locust, cankerworm, caterpiller and palmerworm “my great army which I sent among you”, and 2:11 uses “army” of the Lord’s host. The verses do not say whether the northern army is the insect swarm, a human army or something else. Readings are named as readings only. The published entry `exp-late-28-2-4` compares the army imagery of 2:4 and 2:25; it does not ask who the northern army is, and the record says so. The KJV’s word “army” is the KJV’s; the Hebrew quoted prints only the adjective (הצפוני) and the record does not rest on the Hebrew, so no T was proposed for it (a wording-only addition).
2. `the-captivity-of-jerusalem-which-is-in-sepharad-where-is-sepharad-obadiah-1-20` — the verse names Sepharad as the place of the captivity of Jerusalem and gives no other detail. A whole-KJV word search (whole word, case-sensitive; all 1,189 packaged chapters visited, checked by script) finds the name at this verse only. The Greek of the verse prints a different name; that is the subject of a separate T record, and this record does not rest on it.
3. `until-she-which-travaileth-hath-brought-forth-who-is-she-micah-5-3` — Micah 5:3 ties the giving up to the time “she which travaileth hath brought forth”, after 5:2 speaks of one who comes forth out of Bethlehem; 4:9–10 has the daughter of Zion told to labour like a woman in travail. The verses do not say who she is. Readings are named as readings only; how Matthew 2:6 uses 5:2 is not examined.

## Considered and left out

- Jonah 3:6 “the king of Nineveh” (unnamed): an unnamed king in a narrative is ordinary omitted detail, and the passage names his title and city.
- Jonah 1:17 “a great fish”: the kind of creature is not material to the passage’s meaning; the Greek and the New Testament word “whale” is not examined.
- Amos 9:11 “the tabernacle of David that is fallen”: the verse and 9:12 give the effect (possessing a remnant) and the passage’s own image; the question of what the tabernacle is would rest on how later writers apply it, which is outside this session’s limits.
- Amos 7:14 “I was no prophet, neither was I a prophet’s son”: the verse explains itself with the following “I was an herdman”.
- Micah 5:2 “whose goings forth have been from of old”: the reference is stated in the verse; whether it is a person or a line is interpretive and was not raised as a gap.
- Joel 2:28–29 “all flesh” and “servants and handmaids”: a statement of scope, not a gap in the passage.
- Obadiah 1:1 “an ambassador is sent among the heathen”: the sender and subject are named in the same verse (“from the Lord”).
