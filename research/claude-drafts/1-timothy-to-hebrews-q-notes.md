# 1 Timothy to Hebrews — Q notes (2026-10-14)

All drafts until Codex accepts them. 3 Q records in `1-timothy-to-hebrews-q-records.json`, each with a side A on the key verse:

- `saved-in-childbearing-who-is-she-and-from-what-1-timothy-2-15` — Saved from what, and how, “in childbearing” (1 Timothy 2:15)?
- `he-found-no-place-of-repentance-whose-repentance-and-what-he-sought-hebrews-12-17` — Whose repentance, and what did Esau seek “with tears” (Hebrews 12:17)?
- `jannes-and-jambres-who-were-they-2-timothy-3-8` — Who were Jannes and Jambres (2 Timothy 3:8)?

Each follows the Q contract: the passage leaves the point open, readings are named only as readings, and none is chosen. Absence claims are bounded to the verses quoted; word searches are word searches of the packaged KJV (case-sensitive, whole word: “Jannes”, “Jambres” and “childbearing” occur only at the one verse each). The Greek quoted is Tischendorf (eighth edition) as served by bolls.life, checked against the Textus Receptus text served there.

Considered and left out: 1 Timothy 3:11 (“their wives” / “the women”: the English supplies a possessive; whether the women are wives or servants is a matter of reading the Greek noun, and the question is about office, not an unstated fact); Hebrews 6:4–6 (the impossibility of renewal: the passage states a condition and a result, and the disputed point is theological, not an unstated identity or cause); Hebrews 7:3 (Melchizedek “without father”: covered by the published Q on Melchizedek); Hebrews 4:8 (“Jesus” for Joshua: a name form, not an unanswered question); Titus 1:12 (“a prophet of their own”: the verse names the people, and the writer is not asked about); 2 Timothy 4:13 (the cloke left at Troas: ordinary detail); Philemon 1:15–16 (why Onesimus departed: the verse itself gives the reason offered).
