## Why each qualifies

1. `jews-slew-seventy-five-thousand-or-fifteen-thousand-greek` (9:16) — one figure: 75,000 in Hebrew and KJV, 15,000 in the Greek.
2. `feast-in-shushan-seven-days-or-six-days-greek` (1:5) — one figure: seven days, Greek six.
3. `esther-taken-to-the-king-in-the-tenth-month-tebeth-or-the-twelfth-month-adar-greek` (2:16) — month number and name both differ.
4. `second-letters-written-in-the-third-month-sivan-or-the-first-month-nisan-greek` (8:9) — month number and name both differ; the interval after Haman’s letters changes from over two months to ten days.
5. `day-set-to-destroy-the-jews-thirteenth-of-adar-or-fourteenth-greek` (3:13, 3:7) — the Greek has the lot fall on the fourteenth (3:7), gives no day at 3:13, and names the fourteenth in the lettered verse 3:13f; the KJV has the thirteenth. At 8:12 and 9:1 the three texts agree on the thirteenth.
6. `mordecai-took-esther-for-his-own-daughter-or-brought-her-up-for-a-wife-greek` (2:7) — “for a daughter” in Hebrew and KJV, “for a wife for himself” in the Greek.
7. `esther-the-daughter-of-abihail-or-of-aminadab-greek` (2:15, 9:29) — a different name for Esther’s father, not a spelling.
8. `haman-the-agagite-or-the-bugaean-and-the-macedonian-greek` (3:1, 9:24) — the Greek never has “Agagite”; it has two other descriptions.
9. `god-named-in-the-greek-of-esther-where-the-kjv-names-none` (6:1, 2:20, 4:8, 6:13) — in four verses the KJV also has, the Greek names God or the Lord; a scripted word search finds neither word in the KJV of Esther.
10. `on-the-spoil-laid-they-not-their-hand-or-they-plundered-greek` (9:10) — the Greek has the verb without the negative at 9:10 and with it at 9:15 and 9:16.
11. `king-ahasuerus-in-esther-or-king-artaxerxes-greek` (1:1) — the Greek calls the king Artaxerxes throughout; shown at four verses.

## Points for Codex

- **Additions rule.** Records 5 and 9 rest partly on Greek words the KJV lacks. Each says something the KJV does not state in the same passage: the day on which the lot fell (3:7), and God as actor or as the one to be feared and called upon (2:20, 4:8, 6:1, 6:13). The KJV verses on both sides of each were read.
- **Tag placement.** Each G is placed only on verses where the Greek differs. Agreeing verses (1:10; 3:12; 8:12; 9:1; 9:15; 9:16) are in `refs` only, and each scope says where the tag applies.
- Record 11 sits on Esther 1:1 beside the published question `ahasuerus-and-artaxerxes-between-cyrus-and-darius-which-kings-are-meant`. That record asks which kings are meant in Ezra 4 and does not give the Greek of Esther. This one compares the printed names only and says it does not identify either king. If Codex judges it a duplicate, hold it; nothing else depends on it.
- Record 8: the link from “Agagite” to Agag (1 Samuel 15:8) is given as a reading, conditional, and not as a meaning of the word. No lexicon is cited and none is needed for what the record claims.
- Record 10: Brenton’s English adds “their property”; the Greek page prints no object. Only the Greek is quoted, and the scope says the verb has no object.
- Record 2 overlaps the published `early-esther-susa` at 1:5 by verse only; that record is about the palace remains.
- Brenton’s Greek of 6:1 prints `δε` and `τοὺ` as quoted; the spelling is the page’s own.
- English in square brackets is the researcher’s rendering; no English is attributed to the Greek page.

## Verses compared

All 167 verses were compared between the KJV and Brenton’s English by a number-word script (`numscan.mjs`), and Brenton’s English was read through, including every lettered passage. The Greek was read at: 1:1–5, 1:10; 2:5–7, 2:15–16, 2:19–21; 3:1–7, 3:10, 3:12–13; 4:8, 4:14, 4:17; 6:1, 6:13; 8:3, 8:5, 8:9, 8:12; 9:1, 9:6, 9:10–11, 9:15–16, 9:24, 9:29; 10:1, 10:3. The Hebrew was read at: 1:1, 1:5; 2:5–7, 2:15–16, 2:20; 3:1–2, 3:4, 3:7, 3:13; 4:8, 4:14; 6:1, 6:13; 8:9, 8:12; 9:10, 9:15–16, 9:24, 9:29. The Hebrew of the rest of the book was not read, so no H was looked for outside those verses. Outside Esther: Ezra 4:6–7 (KJV and Greek), 1 Samuel 15:8 (KJV).

## No H, no V, no untagged

- **H:** no verse compared showed a difference of meaning between the Hebrew and the KJV. Two candidates were left out because they would rest on a dictionary meaning with no source supplied: “green” among the hangings (1:6) and “mules, camels, and young dromedaries” (8:10, 8:14).
- **V:** no act told in the book is a clear breach of a law by someone under it that goes unchecked. Esther’s marriage to the king is not within the wording of the marriage laws compared (they name particular nations), and what she ate in the palace is not described.
- **Untagged:** no direct conflict between two KJV statements was found in the verses compared. The time between Jeconiah’s captivity and the reign of Ahasuerus (2:6) depends on who “who” is and on which king is meant, so it is a Q, not a conflict.

## Considered and not issued

- The Greek-internal tensions (the plot of the two chamberlains told in the second year at 1:1m–o and again after the seventh year at 2:21–23; Haman “hanged with his whole family” at 8:12r before the sons are slain at 9:6–10): both sides are Greek only, with no KJV verse to place them on.
- The seven chamberlains and seven princes (1:10, 1:14): different names, and three princes named for seven; names are not identified across texts.
- 127 provinces (1:1) beside the 120 princes of Daniel 6:1: different kings and different units.

## Held back, quotes sourced

Greek differences at 2:21 (the chamberlains grieved “because Mardochaeus was promoted”), 1:1r (Haman’s grudge “because of the two chamberlains”), 8:11 (no leave to slay “little ones and women” or to take spoil; “to use their own laws”), 8:17 (“were circumcised”), 9:5 (verse not printed), 2:19 (no second gathering of the virgins), 6:8 (no crown royal), 9:19–22 and 9:31 (different wording of the feast and the fast), 10:1 (“wrote” for “laid a tribute”), 4:1 (Mordecai’s cry given in words), 7:4 (different wording of Esther’s plea).
