## A. Direct wording or arithmetic

### H1. Chemosh named as the god of Ammon's king

- **Verses:** Judges 11:12–14, 11:24; Numbers 21:29; 1 Kings 11:7, 11:33. No existing ID.
- **Suggested:** id `jephthah-chemosh-and-ammon`, mode contradictions, status Context.
- **Summary:** "Jephthah tells the king of Ammon that Chemosh is 'thy god'. Elsewhere Chemosh is Moab's god and Ammon's is Milcom or Molech."

**Explicit wording [text]**

- 11:12–14: the message is addressed to "the king of the children of Ammon".
- 11:24: "Wilt not thou possess that which Chemosh thy god giveth thee".
- Numbers 21:29: Moab is the "people of Chemosh".
- 1 Kings 11:7: "Chemosh, the abomination of Moab … Molech, the abomination of the children of Ammon". 11:33: "Chemosh the god of the Moabites, and Milcom the god of the children of Ammon".
- The Greek text also reads Chemosh ("Chamos") at 11:24 **[fetched]**, so this is not a copying variant in that tradition.

**Reasoning**

- Taken at face value, the speech assigns Moab's god to Ammon.
- The rest of the speech is also about Moab: the land between Arnon and Jabbok (11:13, 18, 22), Balak king of Moab (11:25), and the statement that Israel took neither Moab's land nor Ammon's (11:15). That makes "a speech originally about Moab" one explanation, and "Ammon then ruled Moabite territory and its cult" another. Neither is stated.

**Interpretation limits**

- It is a character's diplomatic speech, not narrator's description. A speaker can be wrong or be using the local god of the disputed land.
- Do not claim Ammon never worshipped Chemosh; the texts say only whose god he characteristically was.
- The Moabite inscription of King Mesha names Chemosh as Moab's god **[memory]**. It supports Kings and Numbers; it says nothing about Ammon.

**Sources:** https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/JDG11.htm · https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/NUM21.htm · https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/1KI11.htm · https://ebible.org/eng-Brenton/JDG11.htm

### H2. Did Israel take Ammonite land?

- **Verses:** Judges 11:13, 11:15, 11:21–22; Deuteronomy 2:19, 2:37; Numbers 21:24; Joshua 13:25. No existing ID.
- **Suggested:** id `ammonite-land-taken-or-untouched`, mode contradictions, status Context.
- **Summary:** "Deuteronomy and Jephthah say Israel took none of Ammon's land. Joshua gives Gad 'half the land of the children of Ammon'."

**Explicit wording [text]**

- Deuteronomy 2:19: "I will not give thee of the land of the children of Ammon any possession". 2:37: "unto the land of the children of Ammon thou camest not".
- Numbers 21:24: Israel took Sihon's land "unto the children of Ammon: for the border of the children of Ammon was strong".
- Judges 11:15: "Israel took not away the land of Moab, nor the land of the children of Ammon". 11:13: Ammon's king says Israel "took away my land … from Arnon even unto Jabbok".
- Joshua 13:25: Gad's territory includes "half the land of the children of Ammon, unto Aroer that is before Rabbah".

**Reasoning**

- Joshua 13:25 uses the phrase Deuteronomy 2:19 says would not be given.
- The usual reconciliation: the land had been Ammonite, Sihon took it, and Israel took it from Sihon. Numbers 21:26 says exactly this about *Moab's* land **[text]**. No passage says it about Ammon's. Applying it to Ammon is an inference by analogy.
- That inference would also explain why Ammon's king calls it "my land" in 11:13.

**Interpretation limits**

- "Land of the children of Ammon" in Joshua 13:25 may be a geographical name for a district, not a statement of ownership at the time.
- Jephthah's and the king's statements are opposing claims in a dispute; the narrator does not rule on the history.

**Sources:** https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/DEU02.htm · https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/JOS13.htm · https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/NUM21.htm · https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/JDG11.htm

**Unresolved:** Whether this was assessed and declined when Joshua 13 was reviewed (`research/notes/joshua-13-14.md` — I did not re-read it).

### H3. Moab on the journey: refused, supplied, or not approached

- **Verses:** Judges 11:17–18; Deuteronomy 2:9, 2:18, 2:28–29; 23:3–4; Numbers 20:14–21. Existing ID: `deuteronomy-edom-passage-and-provisions` (Edom side only).
- **Suggested:** extend that record, or add `moab-passage-and-provisions`, mode contradictions, status Context.
- **Summary:** "Jephthah says Moab refused Israel passage and Israel stayed outside its border. Deuteronomy says Israel passed through Ar of Moab and that the Moabites of Ar sold provisions; a later law says Moab gave no bread or water."

**Explicit wording [text]**

- Judges 11:17: Israel "sent unto the king of Moab: but he would not consent". 11:18: Israel "came not within the border of Moab".
- Numbers 20:14–21 narrates the request to Edom only; no request to Moab appears there.
- Deuteronomy 2:18: "Thou art to pass over through Ar, the coast of Moab, this day."
- Deuteronomy 2:28–29: Israel asks Sihon to sell food and water and let them pass, "as the children of Esau … and the Moabites which dwell in Ar, did unto me".
- Deuteronomy 23:3–4: Ammonites and Moabites are excluded "because they met you not with bread and with water in the way".

**Reasoning**

- Three statements about Moab: refused (Judges 11:17), did what was asked (Deuteronomy 2:29), and gave no bread or water (Deuteronomy 23:4).
- Two statements about the route: through Ar on Moab's border (Deuteronomy 2:18) and not within Moab's border (Judges 11:18).

**Interpretation limits**

- The existing record already notes that the reach of "did unto me" in 2:29 is ambiguous — it may cover selling food but not granting passage. That limit carries over.
- "Pass over through Ar, the coast of Moab" may mean along the border, which would fit Judges 11:18.
- Deuteronomy 23:4 can be read as "did not come out to offer hospitality", which is compatible with selling for money.
- Jephthah is a character summarising history in a negotiation.

**Sources:** https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/JDG11.htm · https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/DEU02.htm · https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/DEU23.htm · https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/NUM20.htm

### H4. Havoth-jair: named by which Jair, and how many towns

- **Verses:** Judges 10:3–4; Numbers 32:41; Deuteronomy 3:4, 3:14; Joshua 13:30; 1 Kings 4:13; 1 Chronicles 2:22–23. No existing ID.
- **Suggested:** id `havoth-jair-towns-and-naming`, mode contradictions, status Context. Count comparison like `joshua-judah-town-totals`.
- **Summary:** "The 'towns of Jair' are named after a Jair in Moses' day and again after the judge Jair. Their number is given as 23, 30 and 60."

**Explicit wording [text]**

| Passage | Who | Where | How many |
|---|---|---|---|
| Numbers 32:41 | Jair son of Manasseh "called them Havothjair" | Gilead region | not stated |
| Deuteronomy 3:14 (with 3:4) | Jair son of Manasseh; named "unto this day" | Argob, Bashan | sixty (3:4) |
| Joshua 13:30 | "towns of Jair" | Bashan | sixty |
| Judges 10:3–4 | Jair the Gileadite, a judge; thirty sons "had thirty cities, which are called Havothjair unto this day" | Gilead | thirty |
| 1 Kings 4:13 | "towns of Jair the son of Manasseh, which are in Gilead", listed beside sixty cities of Argob | Gilead | not stated |
| 1 Chronicles 2:22–23 | Jair son of Segub | Gilead | twenty-three; sixty with Kenath's towns |

- The Greek text of Judges 10:4 has thirty-two sons, colts and cities **[fetched]**.

**Reasoning**

- Explicit: two different eras for a Jair connected with the name, each with "unto this day"; two regions; three or four counts.
- Judges 10:4 does not say the judge *gave* the name — only that the cities "are called" Havoth-jair. A later Jair inheriting an existing name is consistent with the wording.
- 1 Kings 4:13 separates the towns of Jair in Gilead from the sixty cities of Argob in Bashan, which Deuteronomy 3:14 and Joshua 13:30 run together.

**Interpretation limits**

- Counts may reflect different dates as the holding grew or shrank; no passage says so.
- Whether the judge is the same person as the son of Manasseh is not addressed by any text. By the book's own sequence they are far apart in time.

**Sources:** https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/JDG10.htm · https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/NUM32.htm · https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/DEU03.htm · https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/JOS13.htm · https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/1KI04.htm · https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/1CH02.htm · https://ebible.org/eng-Brenton/JDG10.htm

### H5. Jephthah's vow and the laws on child sacrifice and vows

- **Verses:** Judges 11:29–31, 11:34–40; Deuteronomy 12:31; 18:10; Leviticus 18:21; 27:2–4, 27:28–29; Numbers 30:2; Hebrews 11:32. Existing related IDs: `leviticus-vowed-and-devoted-distinction`, `leviticus-vow-person-valuations`.
- **Suggested:** id `jephthah-vow-and-daughter`, mode contradictions, status Context.
- **Summary:** "Jephthah vows to offer as a burnt offering whatever meets him, and 'did with her according to his vow'. The law forbids burning sons and daughters. The text never describes the act."

**Explicit wording [text]**

- 11:29: "the Spirit of the Lord came upon Jephthah". 11:30–31: whatever comes out "shall surely be the Lord's, and I will offer it up for a burnt offering" (KJV, ASV, WEB all "and").
- 11:34–35: his only child comes out; "I cannot go back".
- 11:39: he "did with her according to his vow … and she knew no man". 11:37–38: she mourns her virginity, not her death. 11:40: an annual lament.
- Deuteronomy 12:31: burning sons and daughters is an abomination the Lord hates. 18:10 and Leviticus 18:21 forbid it.
- Leviticus 27:2–4: a vowed person is valued in money; a female at thirty shekels. 27:28–29: a person "devoted" cannot be redeemed and "shall surely be put to death".
- Numbers 30:2: a man must do all that he has vowed.
- Hebrews 11:32 names Jephthah among examples of faith.

**Reasoning**

- If 11:39 means she was sacrificed, the act is what Deuteronomy 12:31 forbids, done to fulfil a vow that Numbers 30:2 says must be kept; and Leviticus 27:2–4 offered a monetary route he did not use.
- If 11:39 means she was given over to lifelong celibacy, there is no conflict with the sacrifice laws — but then "I will offer it up for a burnt offering" is not what he did.
- The narrator does not describe a killing and does not comment on the vow.

**Interpretation limits**

- The emphasis on virginity (11:37–39) is the main support for the celibacy reading; the plain sense of "burnt offering" and the annual lament support the sacrifice reading. The text does not decide.
- The Greek has "I will offer him up for a whole-burnt-offering" with a personal pronoun **[fetched]**, which leans toward a person being envisaged from the start. English "whatsoever" is a translation choice.
- The KJV margin offers "or I will offer it" **[memory]**; none of the three packaged editions prints "or".
- Being moved by the Spirit (11:29) and being commended in Hebrews do not state approval of the vow.
- The existing Leviticus record already warns against reading 27:28–29 as authorising a private vow to kill. Keep that warning; do not use 27:29 to say the law required her death.

**Sources:** https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/JDG11.htm · https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/DEU12.htm · https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/LEV27.htm · https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/NUM30.htm · https://ebible.org/eng-Brenton/JDG11.htm

**Unresolved:** The Hebrew conjunction in 11:31. Whether the project treats "law forbids X; narrative reports X without comment" as a discrepancy class — this is the same question as the Achan record.

## B. Arguable

### H7. Forty-two thousand Ephraimites killed

- **Verses:** Judges 12:6; Numbers 1:33; 26:37.
- **Explicit [text]:** 42,000 Ephraimites fall at the Jordan fords. Ephraim's fighting men numbered 40,500 at the first census and 32,500 at the second.
- **Reasoning:** The dead in one engagement exceed the whole tribe's earlier muster.
- **Why arguable:** The censuses are generations earlier by the book's own sequence, and populations grow. No passage gives Ephraim's size in Jephthah's day. A comparison of scale, not of two claims about the same thing.
- **Recommendation:** Hold unless a general record on large numbers in Judges is built (see also 8:10 and 20:2).

### H8. "I will deliver you no more" — and then delivers

- **Verses:** Judges 10:13–16; 11:32.
- **Explicit [text]:** 10:13 "wherefore I will deliver you no more". 10:16: Israel puts away its gods and "his soul was grieved for the misery of Israel". 11:32: "the Lord delivered them into his hands".
- **Why arguable:** The narrative itself supplies the turn (repentance, 10:15–16). Existing records on relenting (`exodus-intercession-relenting`, `numbers-blessing-stability-and-relenting`) already frame this pattern.
- **Recommendation:** If adopted, extend `numbers-blessing-stability-and-relenting` with these verses; do not create a new record.

### H9. Past deliverances not narrated; "Maonites" or Midian

- **Verses:** Judges 10:11–12.
- **Explicit [text]:** The list of past oppressors delivered from includes Zidonians and Maonites. Judges narrates no oppression by either. Midian, the most recent major oppressor, is absent.
- **Witness:** The Greek has "Madiam" (Midian) where the packaged editions have "Maonites" **[fetched]**; KJV and WEB agree on Maonites **[text]**.
- **Why arguable:** The missing episodes are silence. The Maonites/Midian difference is a real witness difference of the kind recorded for Joshua 24:12.
- **Recommendation:** Witness note only, if that class is being kept.

- **Limit:** 11:26 is a round number in a speech. It does not fix a date.

## D. Considered and not proposed

- **12:1–2, Jephthah says he called Ephraim.** Chapter 11 mentions no call. Silence; and a character's claim in a quarrel.
- **12:6 Shibboleth.** A dialect difference in pronunciation; nothing to contrast.
- **12:7 "buried in one of the cities of Gilead".** WEB reads "in the cities of Gilead" **[text]**; "one of" is supplied in the KJV. Translation only.
- **11:1 "Gilead begat Jephthah".** Gilead as a person's name beside the region. No conflict.
- **11:3 Jephthah's outlaw band; 12:4–6 killing of fellow Israelites.** Ethical matters.
- **10:6 seven kinds of foreign gods.** A list; nothing to contrast.
- **12:8–15 minor judges' sons, daughters and donkeys.** Nothing to contrast. 12:15 "mount of the Amalekites" in Ephraim is an unexplained place name.
- **11:16–22 against Numbers 21:21–24 on Sihon.** Agreement in substance. No note.
- **11:13 the king's claim "my land".** Covered under H2.
