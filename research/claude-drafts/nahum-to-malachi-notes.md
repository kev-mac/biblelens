# Nahum to Malachi — notes (2026-10-14)

Unit: Nahum, Habakkuk, Zephaniah, Haggai, Zechariah, Malachi (3 + 3 + 3 + 2 + 14 + 4 = 29 chapters). **All records are drafts until Codex accepts them.**

Counts: 48 records in `nahum-to-malachi-records.json`: 45 T (all witness "Septuagint") and 3 untagged; no V; every record carries `sides`. 3 Q in `nahum-to-malachi-q-records.json` (separate notes file).

## Verse numbers (KJV against Hebrew and Brenton)

- Nahum: KJV 1:15 = Hebrew and Brenton 2:1; KJV 2:n = 2:(n+1).
- Zechariah: KJV 1:18–21 = 2:1–4; KJV 2:n = 2:(n+4).
- Malachi: KJV 4:n = Hebrew 3:(n+18). **Brenton’s Greek orders the end differently**: it prints the KJV 4:5 (Elijah) as 3:22, the KJV 4:6 as 3:23 and the KJV 4:4 (the law of Moses) as 3:24, so the law comes last. The Malachi 4:5 record reads Brenton at 3:22.
- Habakkuk, Zephaniah and Haggai agree. Brenton’s pages for the chapter after the last of Nahum, Habakkuk, Zephaniah, Haggai, Zechariah and Malachi answer 404, as expected; Brenton’s Malachi has three chapters, with the KJV’s 4:1–6 as its 3:19–24.

Notes on particular T records:
- Haggai 1:11 (drought / sword): the Leningrad text points the noun as the word for drought, which is the KJV’s reading; the Greek’s “sword” reads the same letters as the word for a sword. So the Greek alone differs, and the witness is Septuagint; the shared consonants are stated in the scope.
- Haggai 2:9 and 2:5 are an addition and an omission in Brenton’s printed Greek. The KJV does not state the added clause anywhere in Haggai 2 (the standing test for Septuagint additions).
- Habakkuk 1:5 and 2:4, Zechariah 11:13, 12:10 and 13:7 carry a New Testament verse as a side B (Acts 13:41, Hebrews 10:38, Matthew 27:10, John 19:37, Matthew 26:31); the record compares Brenton’s Greek of the Old Testament verse with the KJV and does not examine the New Testament use.
- Zechariah 6:10 and 6:14 are one record (names in the KJV, common nouns in Brenton); Zechariah 11:7 and 11:11 are one record (the same Hebrew phrase, rendered by a land and a people); Malachi 3:15 and 4:1 are one record (the same Hebrew word twice); Malachi 3:8 and 3:9 are one record.

## Untagged records

- `spoken-by-jeremy-the-prophet-or-the-thirty-pieces-of-silver-in-zechariah-matthew-27-9` — “spoken by Jeremy the prophet” — are the thirty pieces of silver in Jeremiah or in Zechariah?
- `prepare-the-way-before-me-or-prepare-thy-way-before-thee-malachi-3-1` — “prepare the way before me” (Malachi) or “prepare thy way before thee” (Matthew)?
- `look-upon-me-whom-they-have-pierced-or-look-on-him-whom-they-pierced-zechariah-12-10` — “look upon me whom they have pierced” (Zechariah) or “look on him whom they pierced” (John)?

- Matthew 27:9 is issued here, in the session of the earlier book (Zechariah), under the standing cross-book rule. A search of the packaged KJV for “thirty pieces of silver” finds only Zechariah 11:12–13 and Matthew 26:15, 27:3 and 27:9, and nothing in Jeremiah. The scope says that Matthew could be naming the book under whose name the passage is placed or combining Zechariah with Jeremiah 18, 19 and 32, and that the record does not choose. It is not a hold; it is issued untagged on the same footing as Joel 2:28 with Acts 2:17.
- Malachi 3:1 with Matthew 11:10 and Zechariah 12:10 with John 19:37 are wording contrasts of the same kind, each scoped so that both could stand.

## Verses compared

Every verse of all 29 chapters was read in the KJV against Brenton’s own English (Hebrew numbering mapped), and the Hebrew of every verse quoted was read in the Westminster Leningrad Codex text. The chapters were not otherwise checked word by word against the Greek: where the English of the two matched in meaning, no record was proposed. Every Hebrew word, Greek phrase, Brenton English phrase and New Testament phrase quoted was asserted by script against the fetched pages, and every source address answered.
