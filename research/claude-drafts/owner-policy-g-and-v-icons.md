# Owner policy: G and V icons, text priority

Recorded by Claude · 2026-10-12 · From the project owner's instructions in the Claude session. Draft only; Codex implements.

## What the owner decided

1. **Purpose.** Scripture Lens will be used as a debate tool.
2. **Text priority.** The Greek and the KJV are the two texts that matter. The owner regards the Greek as the source of truth; the KJV is the most commonly read.
3. **ASV.** Kept only for easier reading and may soon be removed. Do not build findings on ASV wording.
4. **G icon.** Marks a place where the Greek text differs from the KJV.
5. **V icon.** Marks a violation of a law, statute or commandment that is **clearly broken** and **clearly unchecked** in the Bible. The person must be **under the Law** at the time. Owner's example: Moses killing the Egyptian is *not* a violation, because he was not under the Law.

## One point the owner should confirm (raised by Claude, not yet answered)

"Greek" means two different things by testament, and a debate opponent will press on it:

- **New Testament:** Greek is the original language. A G note compares the KJV with Greek manuscripts.
- **Old Testament:** the original is Hebrew. The Greek (Septuagint) is an ancient translation, sometimes made from a Hebrew text older than or different from the one behind the KJV. A G note here compares two textual traditions; it does not show the KJV mistranslated an original.

Suggested handling: one G icon, with the details panel stating which case applies ("Greek New Testament" or "Greek Old Testament (Septuagint)").

## Sourcing gap for G notes

Every Greek reading in my drafts comes from **Brenton's English translation** of the Septuagint (ebible.org/eng-Brenton), or, for Acts, from search summaries. For a debate tool the note should cite the Greek wording itself from a named edition. That has not been done. Until it is, G notes should say "Greek, as translated by Brenton".

## Proposed test for V

All three must hold. If any is doubtful, no icon.

| Condition | Passes | Fails |
|---|---|---|
| Under the Law | Israelite, after the law in question was given | Before Sinai; non-Israelites; a rule addressed to someone else (e.g. the king's law applied to a non-king) |
| Clearly broken | The text states the act, and the act is what the law names | The act is not described, is ambiguous, or matches the law only by interpretation |
| Clearly unchecked | No punishment, rebuke or negative comment anywhere in the Bible | The narrator, a prophet or a later book condemns or punishes it |

A fourth case needs the owner's ruling: **acts that break a law but are commanded by God in the story** (e.g. Gideon's sacrifice at Ophrah). They are unchecked, but "violation" may not be the right word. I have marked these "ruling needed".

## What this changes in my drafts

- Edition notes about ASV wording should be ignored. Where a draft says "KJV-only", read it as "KJV; the Greek should be checked".
- From the next batch I will tag each proposal G, V or neither, and check the Greek for every proposal, not only where I happened to look.
