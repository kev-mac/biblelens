# Hebrew and Greek sourcing, batch 1 — Joshua 7 to Judges 11, plus Acts

Researcher: Claude · 2026-10-12 · Draft only; nothing outside `research/claude-drafts/` was changed.

**Owner policy (updated 2026-10-12).** The Hebrew Old Testament and the Greek New Testament are the sources of truth. The Greek Old Testament (Septuagint) is kept as a second ancient witness. Modern translations — the KJV first — are compared *against* those. ASV is for readability only and may be removed.

This file replaces every "[memory]" or "via Brenton's English" claim about Hebrew or Greek wording in my earlier drafts for the verses listed. I retrieved the original-language text myself this session and quote it below.

**Limits of this sourcing**

- I can read these texts and I give plain glosses. I am not a substitute for a lexicon. Where a word's meaning is disputed I say so and do not settle it.
- Brenton's Greek is one form of the Septuagint. In Judges especially, the ancient Greek manuscripts fall into two differing families; a reading here may not be in the other.
- The Septuagint's verse numbers sometimes differ by one from the KJV's (noted where it happens).
- English glosses in quotation marks are mine unless marked KJV.

## 1. Findings that change earlier proposals

### 1.1 Balak "fought": the Hebrew uses the same verb in both passages — strengthens the proposal

| | Hebrew (WLC) | Greek (Brenton) | KJV |
|---|---|---|---|
| Joshua 24:9 | וַיִּלָּחֶם בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל — "and he fought against Israel" | καὶ παρετάξατο τῷ Ἰσραήλ — "and drew up in battle order against Israel" | "warred against Israel" |
| Judges 11:25 | אִם־נִלְחֹם נִלְחַם בָּם — "did he ever fight against them?" | ἢ πολεμῶν ἐπολέμησεν αὐτόν — "or did he ever make war on him?" | "did he ever fight against them" |

- In Hebrew the two verses use the same verb, *laḥam*, "to fight". One says Balak did it; the other asks, expecting the answer no, whether he ever did.
- The KJV hides this by translating one as "warred" and the other as "fight".
- The Greek softens Joshua 24:9 to "drew up against", which is compatible with never actually fighting.
- **Icon: H** on both verses. This is now a same-word contradiction in the source language, which is the form Codex adopts. Proposal: `joshua-21-24-review.md` §B1 and `judges-10-12-proposals.md` H6.
- Limit unchanged: both are speeches by characters.

### 1.2 Gideon's fleece: the same verb as the commandment — changes my earlier "no"

| | Hebrew (WLC) | Greek (Brenton) | KJV |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deuteronomy 6:16 | לֹא תְנַסּוּ אֶת־יְהוָה — "you shall not test the LORD" | Οὐκ ἐκπειράσεις Κύριον | "Ye shall not tempt the Lord" |
| Judges 6:39 | אֲנַסֶּה נָּא רַק־הַפַּעַם — "let me test, please, just this once" | πειράσω δὴ καί γε ἔτι ἅπαξ | "let me prove, I pray thee, but this once" |

- Same Hebrew verb, *nasah*, and the same Greek root, *peirazō*. The KJV's "tempt" and "prove" conceal the match.
- In `owner-policy-g-and-v-icons.md` I marked this "No — doubtful, prove vs tempt". That doubt came from the English. In Hebrew Gideon says he is doing the thing the commandment names.
- **Icon: H**, and it becomes the strongest **V** candidate so far: under the Law, the wording matches, and nothing rebukes him — God grants the request (6:40).
- Limits: Deuteronomy adds "as ye tempted him in Massah", and Massah was a hostile demand, not a request for reassurance. Gideon himself asks God not to be angry, which shows he sees the risk. Owner's ruling needed on whether a verbal match is "clearly broken".

### 1.3 Genesis 48:22: the Hebrew word is "Shechem", and the Greek names the town — confirms a point I had only from memory

| Hebrew (WLC) | Greek (Brenton) | KJV |
|---|---|---|
| וַאֲנִי נָתַתִּי לְךָ שְׁכֶם אַחַד עַל־אַחֶיךָ — "I have given you one *shekhem* above your brothers" | Ἐγὼ δὲ δίδωμί σοι Σίκιμα ἐξαίρετον — "I give you Sikima [Shechem] as a special portion" | "one portion above thy brethren" |

- The Hebrew word *shekhem* means "shoulder" and is also the name Shechem. The Greek translators took it as the town.
- So in the Greek, Jacob says outright that he took Shechem "with my sword and bow", while Genesis 33:19 and Joshua 24:32 say he bought land there.
- **Icons: H and G.** This moves the purchase-versus-conquest comparison in `joshua-21-24-review.md` §A2 from inference to something the Greek states and the Hebrew permits.
- Limit: in Hebrew the word can still be read as "shoulder / ridge / portion". The identification with the town is explicit only in the Greek.

### 1.4 Joshua 24:32 and Genesis 33:19: what Jacob paid, and to whom

| | Hebrew (WLC) | Greek (Brenton) | KJV |
|---|---|---|---|
| Genesis 33:19 | מִיַּד בְּנֵי־חֲמוֹר אֲבִי שְׁכֶם בְּמֵאָה קְשִׂיטָה — "from the sons of Hamor, Shechem's father, for a hundred *qesitah*" | παρὰ Ἐμμὼρ πατρὸς Συχέμ, ἑκατὸν ἀμνῶν — "from Hamor … for a hundred lambs" | "an hundred pieces of money" |
| Joshua 24:32 | מֵאֵת בְּנֵי־חֲמוֹר אֲבִי־שְׁכֶם בְּמֵאָה קְשִׂיטָה | παρὰ τῶν Ἀμοῤῥαίων τῶν κατοικούντων ἐν Σικίμοις ἀμνάδων ἑκατόν — "from the Amorites living in Shechem, for a hundred ewe-lambs" | "an hundred pieces of silver" |

- Hebrew: the same unit, *qesitah*, in both verses. Its value is unknown. The KJV renders it two ways ("money", "silver"). **H**, minor.
- Greek: the price is a hundred lambs, and in Joshua the sellers are "the Amorites living in Shechem", not the sons of Hamor. **G**.
- The Greek of Joshua 24:32 therefore lines up with Genesis 48:22 ("from the Amorite") more closely than the Hebrew does.

### 1.5 Joshua 21:36–37: verified in two Hebrew editions

- WLC (Leningrad): Joshua 21 has **45** verses and includes the Reubenite towns at 36–37 (אֶת־בֶּצֶר … אֶת־יַהְצָה … אֶת־קְדֵמוֹת … אֶת־מֵיפָעַת … עָרִים אַרְבַּע).
- Aleppo edition as served by bolls.life: Joshua 21 has **43** verses. Its verse 36 is the Gad list (וממטה גד …) and the Reubenite towns are absent.
- Greek (Brenton): present, and Bezer carries the refuge label — τὴν πόλιν τὸ φυγαδευτήριον τοῦ φονεύσαντος, τὴν Βοσὸρ.
- Effect: `joshua-21-24-review.md` §B3 said "do not name Leningrad or Aleppo on my word". It can now be said that the two verses are absent from the Aleppo-based edition and present in the Leningrad-based one, citing these two addresses. Without them the Hebrew names 44 towns against a stated 48.
- Limit: this is how one website presents each codex. A printed critical edition should be cited before this is published as a claim about the manuscripts themselves.
- **Icon: H** (a difference between Hebrew witnesses; the KJV follows the longer form).
