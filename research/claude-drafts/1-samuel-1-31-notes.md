Researcher: Claude · 2026-10-12 · **All 22 records are drafts until Codex accepts them.** The `status` field is the intended published value. Nothing outside `research/claude-drafts/` was changed.

Every Hebrew, Greek and KJV quotation was cut by script from the fetched page or `data/chapters.json` and asserted to be an exact run of characters in the stated verse (Greek after removing page markup and collapsing spaces; Hebrew with the edition's own points and accents). All 31 chapters were fetched in WLC, HAC and Brenton Greek, but I compared only the verses listed below, chosen from known difficulties in the book. A verse not mentioned here has not been cleared.

Published records touching 1 Samuel: `early-saul-jabesh` (history; 10:24, 10:27, 11:1–15, 31:11–12) and `early-saul-death` (31:4–5). `nahash-held-his-peace-or-a-month-later` shares 10:27 and 11:1 with `early-saul-jabesh` but is a different finding in a different mode, so it is a new ID. Verse numbers: in 1 Samuel 21 the Hebrew and Brenton Greek run one ahead of the KJV (KJV 21:1 = 21:2). `refs` use KJV numbers; the text of each record names the Hebrew number where it differs.

## Considered and ruled out

- 13:21 "a file": the Hebrew word is disputed and needs an outside source I did not open.
- 6:19 "fifty thousand and threescore and ten men": Hebrew and Greek both have seventy and fifty thousand; no difference. The Greek's "sons of Jechonias" clause not issued — see below.
- 13:5 "thirty thousand chariots": Brenton's Greek agrees.
- 15:29 Greek "Israel shall be divided in two" against "the Strength of Israel will not lie": a real Greek difference, held back to keep the batch small.
- 2:22 Greek lacks the charge about the women at the door; 18:6 Greek has the women meet David, not Saul; 6:19 Greek blames the sons of Jechonias: real Greek differences, held back for the same reason. Say if they are wanted.
- 5:6 and 6:1 Greek additions about mice: the KJV already has the mice at 6:4–5; dropped under the additions ruling.
- 17:50–51 "slew him" twice: a repeated statement, not a contrast; the Greek's lack of 17:50 is mentioned inside the "whose son" record.
- 17:54 Goliath's head taken to Jerusalem: needs an argument about who held Jerusalem; not a wording contrast.
- 14:49 against 31:2 (names of Saul's sons): lists not stated to be exclusive.
- 1:1 Elkanah "an Ephrathite" against the Levite genealogy in 1 Chronicles 6: not a direct wording contrast.
- 8:7 and 12:17 (asking for a king is evil) against Deuteronomy 17:14–15: an assessment, not a contrast.
- 16:14 "an evil spirit from the LORD": ethical.
- 28 (the woman at Endor): Saul is rebuked in 1 Chronicles 10:13, so no V.
- 13:9 Saul's offering; 2:12–17 Eli's sons; 6:19 looking into the ark; 14:32 eating with the blood: each is rebuked or punished in the passage, so no V.
- 19:13 the image in David's house; 7:1 Eleazar set apart to keep the ark; 7:9 Samuel offering: the law broken is not clear. 22:18 the killing of the priests: the story itself condemns it through the servants' refusal. Dropped, not issued untagged.
- 27:7 Hebrew "days" for the KJV's "a full year": the Hebrew may carry that sense; not H.
- 15:35 Hebrew "did not again see" for the KJV's "came no more to see": the Hebrew may carry that sense; not H.

## Question for the owner

`david-shewbread-not-lawful` is the first V record proposed. Matthew 12:4 and Mark 2:26 call David's act "not lawful" but use it as a defence, not a rebuke. Does a later verse that names the act unlawful without condemning it count as the act being "checked"? If yes, drop this record.
