# Psalms 1–41 (Book I) — Q notes

2026-10-13. Three Q records in `psalms-1-41-q-records.json`. All are drafts until Codex accepts them. Each has `mode: "questions"`, `tags: ["Q"]`, `status: "Context"` and a side A on the tagged verse.

Numbering, as in the main notes file: Brenton’s Greek prints KJV Psalms 9 and 10 as one psalm, so KJV Psalms 11–41 are Greek 10–40; where a psalm has a title, the Hebrew and the Greek count it as verse 1 and run one ahead of the KJV. Every Hebrew and Greek quotation is given at its own number, with the KJV number beside it.

## Records

1. `selah-what-does-the-word-tell-the-reader-or-singer` (Psalms 3:2) — an unexplained word printed inside the text 71 times in the Psalms and 3 times in Habakkuk 3; the five verses quoted do not say what it means or what is to be done at it.
2. `thou-shalt-keep-them-who-or-what-is-kept` (Psalms 12:7) — the KJV’s “them” can point to the poor and needy of verse 5 or to the words of the Lord of verse 6; the verse does not say which, and what is promised differs.
3. `in-the-volume-of-the-book-it-is-written-of-me-which-book` (Psalms 40:7) — the speaker appeals to a writing about himself and does not name it; Hebrews 10:7 repeats the phrase and does not name it either.

## Checks made

- “Selah”: whole word, case-sensitive, counted by script in the packaged KJV: 75 (Psalms 71, Habakkuk 3, and 2 Kings 14:7 where it is a place). The five verses quoted were read; the others were counted, not each read.
- “Higgaion”: one verse (Psalms 9:16). “volume of the book”: two verses (Psalms 40:7, Hebrews 10:7).
- Psalm 12 was read in full in the KJV (8 verses). Psalms 40:6–8 and Hebrews 10:5–7 were read.
- The psalms of this part were not read in full; see the main notes file for the verses compared.

## Points for Codex

- Record 2 shares its verse with the G record `thou-shalt-keep-them-or-keep-us-greek`. They ask different things: the G record compares the Greek “us”; the Q record asks what the KJV’s “them” refers to.
- Record 3 shares Psalms 40:6–7 and Hebrews 10:5 and 10:7 with the G record on “a body hast thou prepared me”. The Q is about the book only.
- Record 2 quotes Hebrew and no Greek; its closing limits and `languageContext` name the Hebrew only, as Codex corrected in the Job Q batch.
- No rendering of “Selah” or of the Greek word is given, because none could be given without a dictionary source.

## Considered and not issued

- Psalms 2:7, “this day have I begotten thee” (who, and which day): Acts 13:33 and Hebrews 1:5 and 5:5 give the New Testament’s reading, so it is answered elsewhere.
- Psalms 8:4–5, 16:10, 22 and 41:9 (who is spoken of): the New Testament applies each; a Q would exist only if that application were set aside.
- Psalms 6:5 and 30:9 (the dead and praise): questions put in verse; a Q would rest on reading them as plain statements.
- The psalm titles (Cush the Benjamite, Psalm 7; “Abimelech”, Psalm 34): the packaged KJV does not hold the titles, so nothing in them can be quoted or given a side.
- Psalms 29:1, who the “mighty” are: the “sons of God” question is already published from Genesis 6:2 and Job 1:6.
- Psalms 34:7, the angel of the Lord: already published as a question.
