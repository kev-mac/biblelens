## Checks made

- Packaged KJV read at Psalms 74:7–9, 79:1–2, 82:1–2, 82:6–7, 89:3–4, 89:38–40 and 89:51, and at 2 Kings 25:9 and John 10:34–35. A word search of the packaged KJV for “cast fire into thy sanctuary” returned Psalm 74:7 only. These are word searches and readings of the verses named, not a reading of the whole Bible.
- Hebrew (WLC) quoted at the verses in each record; Greek was not quoted in the Q records. Every address answered.
- Published and drafted records were re-checked just before writing. The only published record naming a verse of this part is `behemoth-and-leviathan-what-creatures-are-they` (74:14), which these Q records do not touch. No published Q covers Psalm 74, 79, 82 or 89. The Q on “sons of God” (Genesis 6:2) is a different question.
- Greek wording differences at 74:8, 79:1 and 89:39 are issued as G records; the Q records ask different questions.

## Verse numbers

Hebrew and Greek number Psalms 74, 79 and 82 as the KJV does; Psalm 89 runs one ahead (KJV 89:38 = 89:39). Brenton’s Greek numbers the psalms one lower (74 = 73, 79 = 78, 82 = 81, 89 = 88).

## Considered and not issued

- **Why the psalmist envies the wicked until “the sanctuary of God” (73:17)**: the psalm states the cause (73:16–17); not a gap.
- **Rahab, Babylon, Philistia, Tyre, Ethiopia “born there” (87:4–6)**: who “this man” is and where; possible Q, left out because the packaged KJV does not hold enough of the verse’s referents to state the gap narrowly, and Psalm 87 is short and the question overlaps with Isaiah 19 and Psalm 68:31 in other sessions.
- **Which confederacy (83:6–8)**: same “occasion unstated” shape as the first record; not issued separately.
- **Who “the man of thy right hand” and “the son of man” are (80:17)**: the Greek G record at 80:15 sets the KJV wording against the Greek; the identity question was left out to keep the Q set to three.
- **“Ethan the Ezrahite” and “Heman” (title of 88, 89)**: titles are not in the packaged KJV.
- **What “Selah” signals (75:3, 76:3 and others)**: answered by the published Selah question in Psalms 1–41.
