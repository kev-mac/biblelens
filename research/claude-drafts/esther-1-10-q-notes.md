# Esther 1–10 — Q records, notes

File: `esther-1-10-q-records.json` (3 records, all `mode: "questions"`, `tags: ["Q"]`, `status: "Context"`). All are drafts until Codex accepts them. Written 2026-10-13.

## Why each qualifies

- `who-had-been-carried-away-with-jeconiah-mordecai-or-kish` (2:6, with 2:5) — an unstated identity: the relative clause follows three forefathers’ names and the verse does not say whom it describes. It decides whether Mordecai himself came from Jerusalem.
- `why-did-mordecai-not-bow-to-haman` (3:2, 3:4) — an unstated cause on which the whole plot turns: the question is put to Mordecai (3:3) and no reply is reported.
- `enlargement-and-deliverance-from-another-place-what-place` (4:14) — an unstated source of help and an unstated cause for the fall of Esther’s house, in the sentence on which her decision rests.

## Points for Codex

- **Lettered Greek verses.** Brenton’s Greek Esther is at `https://ebible.org/grcbrent/ESG{cc}.htm` (not `EST`, which answers 404). It prints added passages under lettered labels. Two Q records quote them: `1:1c` (chapter 1 page) and `4:17d`, `4:17e` (chapter 4 page). On the chapter 1 page the label `1` is the opening of the added passage and the sentence that matches KJV 1:1 is labelled `1s`. The HTML `id` values repeat, so find verses by the printed label.
- In each record the Greek addition states one of the readings. The scope says so, and says that the KJV and the Hebrew edition have no such verse, so the sentence “none stated” is limited to the KJV verses quoted.
- The first record points to the published Ezra question on which king Ahasuerus is, without re-asking it.
- The third record points to the G record `god-named-in-the-greek-of-esther-where-the-kjv-names-none` in the main batch for the Greek of 4:8.
- Word searches are called word searches: “Jeconiah” (seven verses, asserted by script) and “God”, “gods”, “Lord” in Esther (no hits, asserted by script).
- Genesis 23:7 is also cited by the published `captain-of-the-host-of-the-lord-who-is-he`; here it is only an example of bowing to men.

## Verses read

All 167 verses of Esther were read in Brenton’s English (with every lettered passage) and compared with the KJV by a number-word script; the KJV was read at the verses listed below and at every verse a record quotes. For these three records the Hebrew and the Greek were read at 2:5–6, 3:2, 3:4 and 4:14, and the Greek also at 3:3, 3:5–6 and the lettered verses 1:1c, 4:17d–e. Outside Esther: 2 Kings 24:15, Jeremiah 24:1, Genesis 23:7, 1 Samuel 24:8 (KJV only).

## Considered and not issued

- Why Mordecai charged Esther to hide her people (2:10, 2:20): the passage gives the charge without a reason, but nothing later turns on the reason itself.
- “When the virgins were gathered together the second time” (2:19): unexplained, but not consequential; the Greek lacks the clause.
- Why God is not named in the book: an observation about the whole book, not a gap in a passage; the Greek differences are a G record.
- “The book of the chronicles of the kings of Media and Persia” (10:2): a pointer to a source, of the kind already issued several times.
- The months Nisan and Adar and which king Ahasuerus is: already asked in published records.
