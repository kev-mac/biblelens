## A. Direct wording or arithmetic

### G1. Seventy sons killed, and one of the seventy survives

- **Verses:** Judges 8:30–31; 9:2; 9:5; 9:18; 9:24; 9:56. No existing ID.
- **Suggested:** id `gideon-seventy-sons-count`, mode contradictions, status Context. Same kind of count comparison as `joshua-judah-town-totals`.
- **Summary:** "Gideon has seventy sons. Abimelech kills 'seventy persons', yet the youngest of the seventy, Jotham, escapes."

**Explicit wording [text]**

- 8:30: Gideon had "threescore and ten sons of his body begotten".
- 8:31: his concubine "also" bore Abimelech — on the plain reading a seventy-first.
- 9:5: Abimelech "slew his brethren the sons of Jerubbaal, being threescore and ten persons, upon one stone: notwithstanding yet Jotham the youngest son … was left".
- 9:18 (Jotham speaking, alive): "have slain his sons, threescore and ten persons".
- 9:56 (narrator): "slaying his seventy brethren".

**Reasoning**

- If Gideon had seventy sons besides Abimelech and Jotham is one of them, at most sixty-nine died.
- If Abimelech is counted among the seventy, at most sixty-eight died.
- The narrator states seventy killed in the same sentence that says one was left (9:5), so the text itself treats "seventy" as the name of the group, not a body count.

**Interpretation limits**

- Seventy is a conventional round number for a full household elsewhere (Genesis 46:27, seventy of Jacob's house; Judges 12:14, seventy sons and grandsons **[text]**). Reading it as "the whole house" removes the difficulty; the text does not say that is what it means.
- The count is off by one or two. No claim about the event follows.
- 9:5 itself flags the exception, so this is not an oversight the reader has to discover.

**Sources:** https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/JDG08.htm · https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/JDG09.htm

**Unresolved:** Whether 8:30's seventy includes Jotham (the text implies so by calling him "youngest son of Jerubbaal").

### G2. Midian destroyed in Numbers, innumerable in Judges

- **Verses:** Judges 6:1–5; 7:12; 8:10; Numbers 31:7–10, 31:17. Existing ID: `numbers-midian-captives-and-distribution` covers Numbers 31's ethics only.
- **Suggested:** new id `midian-after-numbers-31`, mode contradictions, status Context; or extend the existing record with a clearly separate paragraph.
- **Summary:** "Numbers says Israel killed every Midianite male, burned all their towns and then killed the boys. In Judges, Midian overruns Israel with armies beyond counting."

**Explicit wording [text]**

- Numbers 31:7: "they slew all the males". 31:8: five kings of Midian killed. 31:10: "burnt all their cities". 31:17: "kill every male among the little ones".
- Judges 6:5: Midian came "as grasshoppers for multitude … without number". 8:10: 120,000 swordsmen fell and 15,000 remained.

**Reasoning**

- Read as the whole Midianite people, Numbers 31 leaves no males to found the nation of Judges 6.
- Read as the Midianites of one region — those involved at Peor, near Moab (Numbers 25 **[memory]**) — other Midianite groups remain. Numbers 31 says "the Midianites" without limiting the scope.
- The elapsed time is not stated in either passage. By the Judges figures it is at least two centuries, which allows regrowth of a surviving group but not of an exterminated one.

**Interpretation limits**

- "All" in conquest summaries has stated exceptions elsewhere; the existing `early-conquest` record treats this at length. The same caution applies here.
- Judges 6–8 groups Midian with Amalekites and "children of the east" (6:3, 33; 8:10), so the large figures are for a coalition.
- Neither account is independently attested.

**Sources:** https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/NUM31.htm · https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/JDG06.htm · https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/JDG08.htm

**Unresolved:** Where Numbers 31's Midianites lived; whether Midian was a single people or a league.

### G3. Midianites called Ishmaelites

- **Verses:** Judges 8:22–26, especially 8:24; Genesis 25:1–2; 37:25–28, 37:36; 39:1. No existing ID (no record covers the Genesis 37 alternation either).
- **Suggested:** id `midianites-and-ishmaelites`, mode contradictions, status Context. One record can serve Genesis 37 and Judges 8.
- **Summary:** "Judges explains the Midianites' gold earrings by saying 'they were Ishmaelites'. Genesis traces Midian to Keturah and Ishmael to Hagar, and also uses both names for Joseph's buyers."

**Explicit wording [text]**

- Judges 8:24: the defeated enemy "had golden earrings, because they were Ishmaelites". 8:26, 28 call the same enemy Midian.
- Genesis 25:2: Midian is a son of Abraham by Keturah. Ishmael is Abraham's son by Hagar (Genesis 16:15 **[text]**).
- Genesis 37:28: "Midianites merchantmen" draw Joseph out and sell him "to the Ishmeelites". 37:36: "the Midianites sold him into Egypt unto Potiphar". 39:1: Potiphar bought him "of the hands of the Ishmeelites".

**Reasoning**

- As genealogy, a Midianite is not an Ishmaelite: different mothers, different lines.
- As usage, both Judges 8 and Genesis 37 treat the names as applying to the same people, which suggests "Ishmaelite" could mean a desert trader or nomad in general. That is an inference from usage, not something either passage states.
- Genesis 37:36 against 39:1 is the sharper pair: Midianites sold Joseph to Potiphar; Potiphar bought him from Ishmaelites.

**Interpretation limits**

- Genesis 37:28 can be read as three parties (brothers, Midianites, Ishmaelites) with the Midianites as middlemen; 37:36 and 39:1 still name different sellers to Potiphar.
- Source-critical explanations (two interwoven accounts) are scholarly hypotheses **[memory]**; do not state them as fact.

**Sources:** https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/JDG08.htm · https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/GEN25.htm · https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/GEN37.htm · https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/GEN39.htm

**Unresolved:** Whether Genesis 37 already has, or should have, its own record; this proposal assumes it does not.

### G4. "Depart early from mount Gilead"

- **Verses:** Judges 7:1, 7:3; 6:33. No existing ID.
- **Suggested:** id `gideon-mount-gilead`, mode contradictions, status Context. Lower priority than G1–G3.
- **Summary:** "Gideon's camp is by the well of Harod near the Jezreel valley, west of the Jordan. The fearful are told to leave 'mount Gilead', a region east of the Jordan."

**Explicit wording [text]**

- 6:33: the enemy camps in the valley of Jezreel. 7:1: Gideon camps at the well of Harod, with Midian north of him by the hill of Moreh.
- 7:3: "let him return and depart early from mount Gilead" (KJV; WEB likewise "from Mount Gilead").
- Elsewhere in Judges, Gilead is across the Jordan (5:17 "Gilead abode beyond Jordan") **[text]**.

**Reasoning:** The place named does not fit the place described. Either an otherwise unknown height near Harod bore the name, or the name has been altered in copying. "Gilboa", which overlooks Harod, is a common conjecture **[memory]** with no manuscript support that I know of.

**Limits:** The text does not say the camp was not on a hill called Gilead. Gideon's clan, Abiezer, belongs to Manasseh, which also held Gilead; a clan or muster name is possible. This is a geographical difficulty, not two conflicting statements.

**Sources:** https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/JDG07.htm · https://ebible.org/eng-web/JDG07.htm

**Unresolved:** Any ancient version reading a different name here — not checked.

## B. Arguable — real textual facts, but Codex's standard may exclude them

### G5. The prophet's speech (6:7–10) is missing from a Dead Sea Scroll

- **Verses:** Judges 6:7–10.
- **Type:** witness difference, like the Joshua 24 "twelve kings" already accepted in qualified form.
- **Facts:** The Qumran fragment 4QJudg-a preserves 6:2–6 and 6:11–13 and goes straight from 6:6 to 6:11, without the unnamed prophet's rebuke **[search]** (Trebolle Barrera's edition; Hendel's summary for the Biblical Archaeology Society). Many scholars regard 6:7–10 as a later insertion; how much weight the fragment carries is debated **[search]** (Rofé 2011; Ausloos 2014).
- **In the packaged text:** 6:7 repeats 6:6's "cried unto the Lord", and the speech ends at 6:10 with no response before the angel scene opens at 6:11 **[text]**.
- **Limits:** One small fragment. Accidental omission is possible. This is not a contradiction; it is evidence that the passage circulated in two lengths.
- **Sources:** https://library.biblicalarchaeology.org/sidebar/judges-66-11/ · https://research.dial.uclouvain.be/handle/2078.5/45832 · https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/the-prophetic-speech-in-judges-6710-reconsidered/
- **Recommendation:** Add only if the project is recording witness differences as Context. I did not open these pages.

### G6. Gideon tests God twice; Deuteronomy forbids testing God

- **Verses:** Judges 6:36–40; Deuteronomy 6:16.
- **Explicit:** 6:39: "let me prove, I pray thee, but this once with the fleece", prefaced by "Let not thine anger be hot against me" **[text]**. Deuteronomy 6:16: "Ye shall not tempt the Lord your God, as ye tempted him in Massah" **[text]**. 6:36 concedes God has already said he will save Israel.
- **Reasoning:** A command and an act that the narrative does not condemn. Gideon's own plea shows he regards the request as risky.
- **Limits:** The KJV uses "prove" in Judges and "tempt" in Deuteronomy; whether the Hebrew verbs match I did not check. Deuteronomy's example is Massah, a hostile demand; Gideon's is a request for reassurance. A narrative reporting an act does not endorse it.
- **Recommendation:** Context at most. If Codex has a divine-testing record in mind (its note mentions one), this is an extension candidate.

### G7. The angel of the Lord, and the Lord

- **Verses:** Judges 6:11–23; Exodus 33:20.
- **Explicit [text]:** The visitor is "an angel of the Lord" (6:11, 12, 21, 22); the speaker is "the Lord" (6:14, 16, 23). Gideon fears death because he has seen "an angel of the Lord face to face" and is told "thou shalt not die". Exodus 33:20: "there shall no man see me, and live".
- **Why weak:** Gideon says he saw an angel, not God, so Exodus 33:20 is not contradicted by his words. The alternation of speaker is a feature of several such scenes.
- **Recommendation:** Do not add on its own. If a "seeing God" record is ever built around Exodus 24:10–11, 33:11 and 33:20, list Judges 6:22–23 there.

### G8. Crossing the Jordan twice in the telling

- **Verses:** Judges 7:25; 8:1–4.
- **Explicit [text]:** Ephraim brings the heads of Oreb and Zeeb "to Gideon on the other side Jordan" (7:25) and argues with him (8:1–3). Then "Gideon came to Jordan, and passed over" (8:4).
- **Why weak:** A narrative finishing one thread before returning to the main one. Codex has already treated recap structure as non-contradictory at Judges 2:6–9.
- **Related, also weak:** two pairs of Midianite leaders (princes Oreb and Zeeb; kings Zebah and Zalmunna), and Gideon's motive in 8:18–19 — blood revenge for brothers killed at Tabor, an event not narrated. Silence, not conflict.

### G9. Lapping "as a dog" with "hand to mouth"

- **Verses:** Judges 7:5–6.
- **Explicit [text]:** 7:5 separates those who lap "with his tongue, as a dog lappeth" from those who kneel. 7:6 says the three hundred lapped "putting their hand to their mouth" while the rest knelt.
- **Why weak:** A dog does not use a hand, so the two descriptions of the same men sit awkwardly, and some think the phrase is misplaced **[memory]**. But lapping water from a cupped hand reconciles them without strain.
- **Recommendation:** Do not add.

## C. History leads — not ready, recorded with sources

### G10. Camels "without number" (6:5; 7:12; 8:21, 26)

- A peer-reviewed study places the first significant appearance of domestic camels in the Aravah copper sites no earlier than the last third of the 10th century BCE: Sapir-Hen and Ben-Yosef, *Tel Aviv* 40 (2013), 277–285, DOI 10.1179/033443513X13753505864089 **[search — abstract and press coverage only]**.
- Bearing on Judges: by the book's own figures Gideon is well before the 10th century. But the study concerns camels as pack animals at settled sites in the southern Levant. Judges describes camel-riding raiders from the eastern desert, which is where earlier domestication would be expected **[memory]**.
- The university press release drew conclusions about the Bible that go beyond the paper's abstract **[search]**.
- **Recommendation:** Hold. This meets Codex's "primary publication" bar for the Aravah date, but the step from that to Judges 6 needs an explicit assumption about dating and about Arabian evidence that I have not sourced. It belongs with any future Genesis camel record.

### G11. Shechem's destruction and the temple of Baal-berith (9:4, 45–49)

- Excavation at Tell Balata found a large fortress-temple and a destruction around 1100 BCE, often connected with Abimelech **[memory]**.
- This is agreement, not discrepancy. Not proposed, per the instruction to skip notes where history agrees.
- Edition note: 9:46 is "the god Berith" in the KJV and "Elberith" in the WEB **[text]**, beside "Baalberith" in 8:33 and 9:4. Naming difference only.

### G12. Chronology figures in this batch

- 6:1 seven years; 8:28 forty years; 9:22 three years **[text]**. These belong to the deferred chronology proposal (J4). No new argument.
- 9:22 edition note: KJV "reigned … over Israel"; WEB "was prince over Israel" **[text]**. 9:6 and 9:18 make him king "over the men of Shechem". Scope of rule is unstated; not a conflict.

## D. Considered and not proposed

- **Gideon's altar and sacrifice at Ophrah (6:24–26) vs Deuteronomy 12:13–14.** The sacrifice is commanded in the narrative (6:25–26). Codex has already declined to infer a conflict from an authorised altar (Joshua 22; Bochim deferred). Only 6:24, built on Gideon's initiative, lacks a command — too thin.
- **"I will not rule over you" (8:23) vs many wives, collected gold, a son named Abimelech, and 9:2's assumption that the sons would "reign".** Literary irony. The name's meaning ("my father is king") is Hebrew-based inference.
- **"My family is poor … I am the least" (6:15) vs ten servants (6:27).** Self-deprecation in a commissioning scene.
- **Fleece, fire from the rock, the barley-loaf dream.** Presented as signs; no natural claim to test.
- **Three hundred against 135,000 (8:10).** Large figures with nothing to compare them to. The arithmetic of 7:3 (22,000 leave, 10,000 stay) is internally consistent with an unstated 32,000.
- **"God sent an evil spirit" (9:23).** Divine-agency theme already handled in `exodus-hardening-agency`; a doctrinal comparison with the New Testament would be inference.
- **Punishment of Succoth and Penuel (8:13–17); the ephod (8:27).** Ethical matters; the narrative itself calls the ephod a snare.
- **Jerubbaal / Jerubbesheth (2 Samuel 11:21).** A name-form difference **[text]**; 2 Samuel otherwise agrees with Judges 9:50–54 on the woman, the millstone and Thebez. Assess at 2 Samuel 11 if at all.
- **6:25–30 "grove".** WEB reads "Asherah" **[text]**. Same translation matter raised at 3:7.
- **Tribes summoned (6:35; 7:23–24; 8:1).** Consistent: Ephraim is called only after the rout, which is what it complains about.
