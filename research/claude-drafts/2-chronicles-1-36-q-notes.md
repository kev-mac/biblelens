## Records

1. `a-writing-from-elijah-the-prophet-when-was-it-written-and-how-did-it-come` (21:12) — a writing from Elijah reaches Jehoram and speaks of the killing of his brothers as done; Kings tells of Elijah being taken up before a campaign in which Jehoshaphat is alive. The verses do not say when the writing was made or how it came. The scope says the order of telling in Kings is not shown to be the order of events, and that “poured water on the hands of Elijah” does not by itself say Elijah was gone.
2. `the-words-of-necho-from-the-mouth-of-god-how-did-god-speak-by-necho` (35:22, 35:21) — the narrator calls Necho’s warning “from the mouth of God”; no prophet, dream or sign is named. Two side-A entries.
3. `put-the-holy-ark-in-the-house-where-had-the-ark-been` (35:3) — Josiah tells the Levites to put the ark in the house; the checked verses report its entry under Solomon and nothing between.

## Checks made, and their limits

- Record 1: eight verses read in the KJV; 21:12 in Hebrew and Greek. Word search: “Elijah” occurs in 2 Chronicles only at 21:12 (asserted by script). A word search, not a reading.
- Record 2: 35:20–22 read in KJV, Hebrew and Greek; 2 Kings 23:29 in the KJV.
- Record 3: four verses read in the KJV; 35:3 in Hebrew and Greek. Word search: “ark” occurs in thirteen verses of 2 Chronicles, none between 8:11 and 35:3, and in no verse of 2 Kings (both asserted by script). A word search, not a reading.
- The book was not read in full.

## Points for Codex

- Record 3 states a Greek difference as wording and does not tag it: Brenton’s Greek of 35:3 has “and they put the holy ark in the house” where the KJV and Hebrew have a command. It could be issued as a G record if wanted.
- Records 1 and 2 cite verses that published records also cite (2 Kings 1:17, 2:11, 8:16, 23:29). The questions are different, and each scope says so.
- Record 1’s scope names 2 Kings 1:17 and 8:16 as verses that “may suggest” Jehoram began to reign while his father lived. That is offered as one reading, not as a finding.

## Considered and not issued

- Why the captains of Assyria took Manasseh to Babylon (33:11): a matter of outside history, not a gap in the passage.
- “God left him, to try him” (32:31): a theological premise, not an unstated mechanism.
- Which Zacharias is meant at Matthew 23:35 (beside 2 Chronicles 24:20–22): the gap is in Matthew, so it belongs to that book’s session.
