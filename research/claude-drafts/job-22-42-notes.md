# Job 22–42 — records, notes

File: `job-22-42-records.json` (13 records: 13 G, no H, no V, no untagged; all carry `sides`). All are drafts until Codex accepts them. Written 2026-10-13. The Q records are in `job-22-42-q-records.json`, with their own notes.

Job is done in two parts. This batch is part 2 (the third cycle of speeches, Job’s closing speeches, Elihu, the Lord’s speeches and the epilogue) and finishes the book.

## Verse numbers

- **Chapters 40–41 are numbered differently.** KJV 40:1–24 = Hebrew (WLC) and Brenton’s Greek 40:1–24. KJV 41:1–8 = their 40:25–32. KJV 41:9–34 = their 41:1–26. The leviathan record quotes KJV 41:1 from the chapter 40 pages at verse 40:25 and says so. The script asserts the counts (KJV 24 and 34 verses; Brenton 32 and 26 labels).
- In every other chapter of 22–42 WLC has the KJV’s verse count.
- Brenton’s Greek prints no 23:14 and no 35:3. It prints lettered verses `15a` in chapter 23, `28a` and `28b` in chapter 36, and `17a`–`17e` after 42:17. The script asserts that the labels of chapter 42 end `16 17 17a 17b 17c 17d 17e`.
- Brenton’s Greek Job is at `https://ebible.org/grcbrent/JOB{cc}.htm`. All 25 source addresses of the two files answered.

## Why each qualifies

- `morning-stars-sang-together-or-when-the-stars-were-made-greek` (38:7) — G: no singing stars, and “all my angels” for “all the sons of God”.
- `smiteth-through-the-proud-and-crooked-serpent-or-sea-monster-and-apostate-dragon-greek` (26:12, 26:13) — G: a sea monster for “the proud”; a dragon killed where the KJV has a serpent formed; no spirit garnishing the heavens.
- `dead-things-are-formed-from-under-the-waters-or-giants-greek` (26:5) — G: “giants” for “dead things”, and a question for a statement.
- `multiply-my-days-as-the-sand-or-as-the-stem-of-a-palm-tree-greek` (29:18) — G: no nest and no sand in the Greek.
- `a-messenger-an-interpreter-one-among-a-thousand-or-a-thousand-death-bearing-angels-greek` (33:23) — G: a thousand death-bearing angels for one messenger among a thousand; fault and folly for uprightness.
- `behold-now-behemoth-or-wild-beasts-and-played-with-by-his-angels-greek` (40:15, 40:19) — G: plural “beasts” and no clause of making at 40:15; angels and no sword at 40:19.
- `canst-thou-draw-out-leviathan-with-an-hook-or-a-dragon-greek` (41:1) — G: a common noun where the KJV has a name; nose and halter for tongue and cord.
- `i-abhor-myself-and-repent-in-dust-and-ashes-greek-has-no-repenting` (42:6) — G: no verb of repenting in the Greek.
- `not-spoken-of-me-the-thing-which-is-right-like-my-servant-job-or-against-my-servant-job-greek` (42:8, 42:9) — G: “against my servant Job” at 42:8, with a clause about destruction; the friends’ sin pardoned at 42:9 where the KJV has Job accepted.
- `a-piece-of-money-and-an-earring-of-gold-or-a-lamb-and-four-drachms-of-gold-greek` (42:11) — G: different gifts.
- `jemima-kezia-and-kerenhappuch-or-day-casia-and-amaltheas-horn-greek` (42:14) — G: two of the three names are ordinary Greek words.
- `job-lived-an-hundred-and-forty-years-or-a-hundred-and-seventy-greek` (42:16) — G: 170 for 140, and an added total of 240.
- `so-job-died-being-old-and-full-of-days-greek-adds-an-ending` (42:17) — G: five added passages that state things the KJV verses compared do not (rising again, the name Jobab, parents, wife and son, a list of kings, the friends as kings).

## Points for Codex

- **The Greek ending is one record.** It quotes all five lettered passages in full, each by its printed label on `https://ebible.org/grcbrent/JOB42.htm`. The part 1 question `a-man-in-the-land-of-uz-where-and-when-did-job-live` already quotes 17b and part of 17c; this record is the G comparison for 42:17 and does not repeat that question. If Codex would rather have the sentence about rising again (17a) as its own record, say so and it will be issued separately.
- **Additions rule.** The ending was checked against the KJV of Job 1:1–5, 2:11 and 42:7–17 and against Genesis 36:32–35; the KJV states none of the added matter in the same passage. The scope says that the KJV of Genesis has a Jobab among the kings of Edom and does not call him Job.
- **Four English quotations from Brenton’s own English pages stand in scopes**, each with its address (`eng-Brenton/JOB29.htm`, `JOB40.htm`, `JOB42.htm` twice): “the stem of a palm-tree”, “the serpent”, “even of unstamped gold”, and “he called the first, Day, and the second, Casia, and the third, Amalthæa's horn” (printed there with a straight apostrophe). The script asserts each against the fetched page. These addresses are in the scope text and not in `sources`; all three answered.
- **Greek printed without accents.** Brenton’s page prints `χιλιοι αγγελοι` at 33:23 without accents and `φορβαίεαν` at 40:25; both are quoted as printed.
- **No H anywhere.** Each record that quotes Hebrew says that it does not establish what the Hebrew word means in the verse (26:5, 26:12, 42:6, 42:11). The Hebrew is there so the three texts stand together.
- **42:7 carries no tag** in the 42:8–9 record: the Greek of 42:7 has “as my servant Job” like the KJV. It is in `refs` only.
- **Overlaps reported by the script.** Job 38:7 is a context verse of two published Q records (`sons-of-god-and-daughters-of-men-identity-unstated`, `god-speaks-as-us-addressees-unstated`) and Job 1:6 of part 1 drafts; Job 42:7 carries the published `exp-late-17-42-7`; Job 26:7, next to the 26:5 and 26:12–13 records, carries the published `exp-late-17-26-7`. None compares the Greek at these verses.
- **Typing.** The packaged KJV was quoted as packaged; no misprint was met at the verses quoted.

## Considered and not issued

- 22:30, “the island of the innocent” — an H candidate (the Hebrew has two short words); it would need a dictionary source to show a difference of meaning, and the Greek has no island either way. Left out.
- 26:12, “the proud” for the Hebrew word — not tagged H: the KJV rendering a word otherwise elsewhere does not show its meaning here. The Greek difference is issued.
- 38:31–32, the star names (Pleiades, Orion, Mazzaroth, Arcturus) — names of stars are not compared across texts; the Greek has “the evening star” for “Arcturus”. Held back.
- 39:13, “peacocks” and “ostrich” — the Greek wording was looked at once and not examined; identifying birds across texts is not done. Left out.
- 35:4, “thy companions” / “thy three friends” — small; held back.
- 42:7, “My wrath is kindled against thee” / “Thou hast sinned” — held back; the 42:8–9 record quotes the end of the verse.
- 42:10, the Greek adds that the Lord forgave the friends their sin — the same statement as the Greek of 42:9, which is issued.
- 23:14 and 35:3 not printed in Brenton’s Greek — the neighbouring Greek verses were not checked for the same matter under other numbers, so neither absence is issued.
- 36:28a–b and 23:15a, Greek pluses — verse of general content; nothing set against a KJV statement. Held back.
- 42:11, “all the evil that the Lord had brought upon him” beside 2:7, where Satan smites Job — a conflict between the two parts of the book belongs to the part 1 session, which is finished. Left out.
- 42:7 beside 38:2 as an untagged conflict — the two statements need not conflict; issued as a question instead.
- V — none: Job is not placed under the Law in the book.
- Statements of a speaker in verse set against another book (for example 26:7, 37:18, 38:4–7) — not issued; poetry is not read as science, and the published `exp-late-17-42-7` says the book corrects its own speakers.
