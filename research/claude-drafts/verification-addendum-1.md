## Now confirmed

### 1. Greek 1 Kings 6:1 reads 440, not 480 (bears on J4)

- Brenton's Greek-based translation gives "the four hundred and fortieth year" after the exodus **[fetched]**.
- The packaged KJV gives the 480th **[text]**.
- Effect on J4: there are two transmitted totals, 480 and 440. The summed Judges figures exceed both. Attribute the 440 to an English translation of the Greek, as Codex did for the Joshua 24 "twelve kings".
- Source: https://ebible.org/eng-Brenton/1KI06.htm

### 2. A king of Hazor with a Jabin-type name is attested centuries earlier (bears on J1)

- An 18th-century BCE Mari text records tin shipped to "Ibni-Addad king of Hazor"; the West Semitic form would be Yabni-Haddad, of which Jabin is a short form **[search]** (Biblical Archaeology Society; Encyclopedia.com "Jabin").
- A cuneiform letter fragment found at Hazor, Old Babylonian in date, is addressed "To Ibni" **[search]**. Published by Horowitz and Wasserman, *Israel Exploration Journal* 50 (2000) **[search]**.
- Limit stated by a German Bible Society article: whether the Hazor letter's Ibni is the Mari Ibni-Addad is not settled **[search]**.
- Effect on J1: this supports "Jabin was a recurring royal name at Hazor" as a real possibility. It does not attest either biblical Jabin; the texts are roughly 500 years older than any proposed conquest date.
- Sources: https://old.biblicalarchaeology.org/node/121533 · https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/jabin · https://www.die-bibel.de/stichwort/22007
- I did not open these pages. Codex should fetch one before citing.

### 3. Acts 7:16 — the edition difference is a real Greek difference (bears on the burial record)

- A critical Greek text (SBL edition) ends the verse "παρὰ τῶν υἱῶν Ἑμμὼρ ἐν Συχέμ" — "from the sons of Hamor *in* Shechem" **[search]**.
- Printed Textus Receptus editions end "… Εμμορ τοῦ Συχέμ" — "Hamor *of* Shechem" **[search]**, which the KJV expands with a supplied "the father".
- So ASV "in Shechem" and KJV "the father of Sychem" follow different Greek wording, not just different English.
- Still unverified: which manuscripts carry each reading. Two searches found no apparatus. Do not name codices.
- Sources: https://www.biblestudytools.com/sblg/acts/7-16.html · https://textusreceptus.thekingsbible.com/Interlinear/44007016

## Confirmed only in part

### 4. Acts 13:20 — two placements of the 450 years (bears on J4)

- Confirmed: KJV-type texts attach "about 450 years" to the judges; ASV, ESV, NIV and similar attach it to the period before them, usually counted as 400 + 40 + 10 **[fetched]** (textandtranslation.org; the author defends the KJV reading).
- Confirmed in the package: KJV and WEB differ exactly this way **[text]**.
- Not confirmed: manuscript names. The page speaks only of "Alexandrian" and "Textus Receptus".
- Recommendation: state the edition difference from the packaged text and stop there.
- Source: https://www.textandtranslation.org/the-acts-1320-problem/

### 5. Date of the Song of Deborah (bears on J2, J3)

- A majority view dates the poem to the 12th–11th century BCE, often calling it among the earliest Hebrew poetry **[search]**.
- A minority argues its vocabulary and grammar are late and that it is a later composition; TheTorah.com's "Dating Deborah" makes that case from, among other things, the relative pronoun in 5:7 **[search]**.
- Recommendation: J2 and J3 should not lean on the poem being older. Their case is the wording of the two chapters. If the date is mentioned at all, say it is disputed and cite both sides.
- Sources: https://www.thetorah.com/article/dating-deborah · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judges_5 (overview only)

### 6. Iron chariots (J8)

- Confirmed that a named specialist holds the sceptical view: Naama Yahalom-Mack, archaeometallurgist at the Hebrew University, quoted as saying iron chariots "did not exist in the Iron Age at all, certainly not in the Iron I" **[search]** — but the quotation reaches me only through an advocacy site that argues against her.
- Her peer-reviewed work on the bronze-to-iron transition in Canaan exists (with Adi Eliyahu-Behar, *Radiocarbon*, 2016) **[search]**; I have not read it and cannot say it mentions chariots.
- The opposing case (iron-fitted, not iron-built) is the Biblical Archaeology Society sidebar **[fetched]**.
- Recommendation unchanged: hold. Codex's own note already declines to assert anachronism without proper sources, and I agree. The honest state is "disputed; primary source for the sceptical quotation not located".
- Sources: https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/transition-from-bronze-to-iron-in-canaan-chronology-technology-and-context/91AC076476A9530F5D672E0BB13CA451 · https://library.biblicalarchaeology.org/sidebar/did-the-canaanites-really-have-iron-chariots/
