## Evidence and decisions

- Flood: expanded at the owner's request to five separately sourced fields: sedimentology/stratigraphy (Suigetsu), glaciology/paleoclimate (Greenland), botany/dendrochronology (Hohenheim), population genetics, and archaeology (Madjedbebe). The assessment concerns recent global inundation and destruction of terrestrial humanity under ordinary processes. It does not assert that Genesis says every aquatic organism or microorganism died. Each field explains its evidentiary limits; an old object or deep lineage alone does not exclude a later flood.
- Population replacement: strengthened the original genome-diversity comparison with Hawks et al.'s bottleneck analysis. Neither effective population size nor a deep lineage is presented as a literal headcount or an exact eight-person simulation. No universal claim excluding every ancestral couple is made.
- Creation age: NASA's approximately 4.5-billion-year Earth and Richter et al.'s 315 ± 34 thousand-year associated fossil chronology contradict a young-Earth interpretation. Genesis itself does not print a creation date. Existing creation-sequence records stay separate.
- Rods: Zhang et al.'s sheep ASIP experiment supports a genetic contribution to pigmentation. Disagrees applies to seeing rods as an inherited-pattern mechanism, with selection and Genesis 31's divine attribution retained. The experiment did not test ancient rods.
- Lifespans: Barbi et al.'s validated age-105-plus cohort and mortality debate justify Context, not an absolute biological ceiling or a retrospective probability calculation. Full article read through PMC with a non-PDF query response; no claim that modern cohorts verify ancient people.
- Camels: opened the regional study's institutional abstract and full paper. First significant Aravah appearance is not the earliest conceivable domestic camel anywhere. Context, not categorical disproof.
- Philistines: opened Feldman et al.'s primary-paper abstract and figures. Ashkelon is not Gerar; retrospective terminology and limits of a genetic sample stay visible. Context.
- Famine: opened the Brooklyn Museum archival account and the 2021 Egyptological volume. The late Famine Stela does not independently identify Joseph. Seven-year grain arithmetic is an explicitly hypothetical bound with stated production, consumption, loss and export assumptions, not a proof of impossibility. Context.
- Land policy: read Guérin's printed pp. 124–125 in the 2021 volume. Royal theoretical ownership differs from practical tenure; tax percentages and priestly exemptions varied. No claimed independent confirmation of Joseph's nationwide purchase. Context.

All scientific and historical URLs were opened in the research session. Some canonical sites intermittently rejected automated requests; publisher PDFs, institutional copies and the same publisher's cookie-error rendering supplied the readable material. Failed URLs were not used as evidence. Scripture quotations come from the packaged KJV; there are no new Hebrew/Greek findings, language tags, sides or changes to scripture.
