## 3.10 — Jeremiah 17:26

Brenton’s cached Greek prints μάννα. The quick check supports treating this as the offering transliteration rather than evidence of wilderness food. The decision is to DROP under the wording-only exclusion, not to establish what every Greek witness intended.

- [GreekDoc’s own lexicon](https://greekdoc.com/lexicon/man.html), entry μαναά, labels the form a transliterated noun meaning gift/grain offering.
- [Korchagin’s original study](https://www.philol.msu.ru/~ref/2015/2015_KorchaginAO_diss_10.02.14_82.pdf), section II.3.2.4 (printed p. 82 onward), documents offering transliterations in historical/prophetic books (e.g. 2 Kings 8:8–9 and Ezekiel 46), with no such transliterations in the Torah. Leviticus therefore does not settle Jeremiah’s usage.
- [The synoptic Jeremiah study’s introduction](https://www.ciando.com/img/books/extract/3788729961_lp.pdf), printed p. 5, note 14 (PDF page 14), specifically includes Jeremiah 17:26 and 41:5 among transcribed words, referring to Pietersma/Saunders. Its extracted Greek font encoding is damaged; it is used for the author’s prose classification, not as an exact Greek quotation.
- [CCAT’s primary apparatus at Baruch 1:10](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/gopher/text/religion/biblical/lxxvar/4Prophets/48%20Baruch.html) records the μαναα / μανναα / μαννα forms and explicitly cross-references Jeremiah 17:26.

Inference: together these checks support the transliteration explanation; identical spelling to the food word alone does not demonstrate a different offering. This is not a new finding or a manuscript reconstruction. No primary critical lexicon entry specific to Brenton’s accented μάννα was available; the decision rests on usage and the cited transcription study.
