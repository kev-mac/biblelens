# New Testament annotation expansion

## What has been researched?

### Takeaway
20 original annotations link 178 verse locations across all 27 New Testament books. This is selected-passage coverage, NOT a completed review of all 260 chapters. Primary biblical texts support the comparisons; no unsupported archaeological or scientific agreement claims are introduced.

### Cited Findings
- **Judas and the field of blood**: Matthew and Acts give different accounts of Judas's death and the field purchase. The narratives differ in agent and manner of death. Combining them requires an unstated sequence, such as a fall after hanging and purchase on Judas's behalf. — [Matthew 27 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/MAT27.htm); [Acts 1 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/ACT01.htm)
- **Approaching or leaving Jericho?**: Mark places the blind man's healing on departure from Jericho; Luke places it on approach. The shared request, crowd rebuke, and healing make these meaningful parallel accounts with a geographical difference. — [Mark 10 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/MRK10.htm); [Luke 18 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/LUK18.htm)
- **Different qualifications to divorce teaching**: The passages share a restrictive view of divorce but state different qualifications. The texts discuss related but not identical situations; treating one sentence as the entire New Testament rule loses relevant qualifications. — [Mark 10 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/MRK10.htm); [Matthew 19 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/MAT19.htm); [1 Corinthians 7 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/1CO07.htm)
- **Resurrection evening and Pentecost**: John and Acts present different scenes for receiving the Spirit. A single-event reading encounters a timing difference; a staged-gift or symbolic-action reading treats the scenes as distinct. — [John 20 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/JHN20.htm); [Acts 1 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/ACT01.htm); [Acts 2 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/ACT02.htm)
- **Faith and works in Romans and James**: Romans rejects justification by works of the law; James rejects faith without action. The wording is in tension, but the immediate arguments target different problems: reliance on law and an inactive profession of belief. — [Romans 3 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/ROM03.htm); [James 2 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/JAS02.htm)
- **A collection with accountability**: The letter describes organized material aid and precautions over handling it. This is primary textual evidence for the practices the letter advocates; its appeal to manna applies an older narrative to resource sharing. — [2 Corinthians 8 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/2CO08.htm); [Exodus 16:14â€“20 (manna narrative)](https://ebible.org/kjv/EXO16.htm)
- **Abraham's descendants and Christ**: Galatians interprets the promise to Abraham through Christ and then extends it to believers. This is a theological rereading of a promise about descendants; the chapter itself uses both an individual and a collective application. — [Galatians 3 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/GAL03.htm); [Genesis 15:1â€“6 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/GEN15.htm)
- **Instructions within a slaveholding household**: These instructions address masters and servants within an existing hierarchy. The passages regulate relations within the hierarchy rather than explicitly order its abolition. The specific commands differ in emphasis. — [Ephesians 6 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/EPH06.htm); [Colossians 4 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/COL04.htm); [1 Peter 2 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/1PE02.htm)
- **Illness among Christian coworkers**: These letters acknowledge serious and continuing illness among coworkers. The texts do not depict every believer or coworker as immediately cured. They preserve different outcomes without supplying medical diagnoses. — [Philippians 2 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/PHP02.htm); [2 Timothy 4 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/2TI04.htm)
- **Shared names and circulating letters**: Colossians and Philemon name overlapping people, offering a textual link between the letters. Shared names support comparison of the social networks represented in the writings; the texts alone cannot establish every biography or resolve authorship debates. — [Colossians 4 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/COL04.htm); [Philemon 1 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/PHM01.htm)
- **Expectation and prerequisites for the return**: First Thessalonians comforts readers about the dead; Second Thessalonians warns against premature claims about the day. Their emphases differ, but neither passage supplies a calendar date. The language of expectation should not be converted into a dated prediction without further argument. — [1 Thessalonians 4 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/1TH04.htm); [2 Thessalonians 2 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/2TH02.htm)
- **Teaching restrictions and women's prophecy**: First Timothy restricts women's teaching; other passages envision women praying or prophesying. A blanket claim that all female religious speech is forbidden does not capture all these passages. Distinguishing prophecy from authoritative teaching is one interpretive response. — [1 Timothy 2 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/1TI02.htm); [1 Corinthians 11 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/1CO11.htm); [Acts 2 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/ACT02.htm)
- **Grace and practical assistance**: Titus distinguishes salvation from righteous works while also requiring useful good works. The local context prevents treating the rejection of salvation by works as a rejection of ethical action. — [Titus 3 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/TIT03.htm); [James 2 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/JAS02.htm)
- **Onesimus: brotherhood and freedom**: Philemon asks for Onesimus to be received as a brother but does not explicitly record a completed emancipation. A request for brotherly reception is present; a formal legal release and its outcome are not narrated. — [Philemon 1 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/PHM01.htm)
- **Jeremiah's promise interpreted in Hebrews**: Hebrews uses Jeremiah's new-covenant promise to argue that the first covenant is becoming obsolete. The later text makes a Christian interpretive move from the word new to the status of the earlier covenant. — [Hebrews 8 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/HEB08.htm); [Jeremiah 31:31â€“34 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/JER31.htm)
- **Explaining the delayed return**: Second Peter explicitly addresses objections that the promised return has not occurred. Reading the comparison as a fixed mathematical conversion for dating creation or the end ignores its function as an answer to delay. — [2 Peter 3 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/2PE03.htm)
- **Confession and claims about sinlessness**: First John warns against denying sin, yet also says those born of God do not sin. An absolute sinlessness reading creates tension. A reading about settled conduct versus individual failures is one way to interpret the passages together. — [1 John 1 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/1JN01.htm); [1 John 3 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/1JN03.htm)
- **Hospitality and disputed teachers**: Second John restricts hospitality to certain teachers; Third John condemns refusal to welcome approved workers. Their commands are not unconditional opposites: they evaluate different visitors. The important question is who the author regards as a legitimate representative. — [2 John 1 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/2JN01.htm); [3 John 1 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/3JN01.htm)
- **Clouds, trees, waves, and wandering stars**: Jude uses natural images to criticize people, rather than give a scientific account of weather or astronomy. The referents are human opponents and their conduct; treating every image as a physical proposition mistakes the passage's rhetorical function. — [Jude 1 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/engwebp/JUD01.htm)
- **Stars and lamps explicitly interpreted as symbols**: Revelation itself explains its seven stars and seven lampstands symbolically. The explicit explanation supplies a strong contextual reason not to read these particular stars as a claim about stellar size or physical placement. — [Revelation 1 (primary text)](https://ebible.org/kjv/REV01.htm)

### Inferences
The interpretation limits in every JSON record identify possible harmonizations, the limits of internal documentary evidence, or metaphorical genre. These are editorial readings of cited primary texts. Context records should never be displayed as conclusive contradictions or scientific confirmations merely because their mode is contradictions or science.

### Gaps
There is no comprehensive external-source or manuscript review in this batch. Source text was read from the packaged KJV chapter records and all linked online source pages were checked using web tools. The KJV Jude page returned an internal tool error; the successful WEB Jude page is used instead. No content from theological study resources was copied.

## How should the batch be integrated?

### Takeaway
Use nt.json as an additive research batch, deduplicate against current annotations by passage and subject, and retain the exact scope qualifications.

### Cited Findings
The sources for every proposed record are embedded in that record and listed above. Internal verse existence checks passed for all 178 locations.

### Inferences
Multiple verse links belong to one contextual finding; they must not inflate the count of unique findings. The presence of a tag in every book must not be presented as exhaustive Bible coverage.

### Gaps
No repository source edits or publication were performed by this researcher. UI behavior and existing duplicate findings require coordinator review. No user-facing review-status labels should be introduced.
