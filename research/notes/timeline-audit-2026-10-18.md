# Timeline chronology audit — 18 October 2026

The owner explicitly requested an independent historical audit in this chat. That authorization applies to this work; the standing Claude research role, schedules and control files were not changed.

The initial checkout contained the newly generated timeline component but no timeline catalog or app route. A targeted chronology screen covered the 1,692 existing annotations (including 112 in history) and all 25 Genesis story entries. The stories contain no absolute-date fields. Ten chronology cases were selected for source comparison or a no-new-flag decision; this does **not** certify an exhaustive historical review of every biblical event or every annotation. The machine-readable inventory lists the screened history IDs, story entries and selected decisions.

Three new history annotations were appended. All 1,692 existing evidence objects, including their summaries, remained unchanged. Six cards have qualified History flags: three existing findings and three additional findings. Four cards explicitly distinguish corroborated setting or missing independent dates from a demonstrated discrepancy. The ten cases appear in narrative order rather than claiming a single fixed absolute chronology.

| Event | Decision | Source and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Patriarchal camels | Retain existing possible-anachronism finding; expose flag | [Sapir-Hen and Ben-Yosef (2013)](https://cris.tau.ac.il/en/publications/the-introduction-of-domestic-camels-to-the-southern-levant-eviden/), author abstract and publication metadata checked. Substantial Aravah use is later than a traditional Bronze Age setting; this does not exclude every earlier individual camel or date Abraham. |
| Patriarchal Philistines | Retain existing possible-anachronism finding; expose flag | [Feldman et al. (2019)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6609216/), abstract, introduction and results checked. Ashkelon’s cultural/ancestry transition is early Iron Age. The study neither dates Abimelech nor excavates his encounter; retrospective naming remains possible. |
| Joseph’s famine | No new dating flag | No absolute date is supplied. Retain the published evidence and its limits; resemblance to a later famine story cannot independently date Joseph. The existing Egyptological assessment was carried forward rather than newly certified in full. |
| Joseph’s land policy | No new dating flag | No absolute date is supplied. The existing annotation already says parallels do not establish this national policy. Missing corroboration alone does not supply a conflicting event date. |
| Jericho’s fall | **New conditional date-conflict flag** | [Bruins and van der Plicht (1995), pp. 213, 218–219](https://pure.rug.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/6807335/1995RadiocarbonBruins.pdf), primary publication opened and read. Their grain measurement is 3306 ± 7 radiocarbon BP; the published preliminary calibrations place that destruction earlier than the proposed c.1400 BCE identification. Use the paper’s broad range, not a fabricated exact year. The sampled layer does not establish the date of every destruction or the absence of later settlement. |
| Sennacherib’s Judah campaign | No new dating flag | Retain the existing 701 BCE campaign reference. The [British Museum’s indexed primary catalog description](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/image/426311001) supports that campaign setting; direct catalog access returned 403 during this audit. This does not resolve all Hezekiah regnal synchronisms, tribute totals or miraculous narrative claims. |
| Darius the Mede | **New succession-problem flag** | [Nabonidus Chronicle iii.15–20](https://www.livius.org/sources/content/mesopotamian-chronicles-content/abc-7-nabonidus-chronicle/), scholarly translation opened, and [Richard N. Frye’s signed Iranica entry](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/darius-ii/) checked. A separate Median predecessor does not fit the recorded Cyrus takeover in 539 BCE. Proposed identification with Cyrus or an official must remain visible; this is not a claim that silence disproves every possible identification. |
| Cyrus and the return | No new dating flag | [Cyrus Cylinder, especially lines 30–33](https://www.livius.org/sources/content/cyrus-cylinder/cyrus-cylinder-translation/), scholarly translation opened. Restoration language supplies context but does not name Judah, Jerusalem or Ezra’s return. The conventional first-year date is a reconstruction, not an independently confirmed date for this particular proclamation. |
| Quirinius’s census | Retain existing conditional chronological-conflict finding; expose flag | [Josephus, Antiquities 17](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/josephus/ant-17.html), [18.1–2](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/josephus/ant-18.html) and [NABRE Luke 2:1–2 scholarly note](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/luke/2) opened. The provincial census in 6–7 CE conflicts with a birth before Herod’s conventionally dated death in 4 BCE if the accounts describe that census. Whiston’s bracketed editorial years are not treated as Josephus’s own chronological wording. |
| Theudas | **New identity-dependent chronology flag** | [Josephus, Antiquities 20.5.1 (§§97–99)](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/josephus/ant-20.html) read directly from the university-hosted page after web extraction failed; [Antiquities 18.1.1](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/josephus/ant-18.html) and [NABRE Acts 5:36–37 scholarly note](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/acts/5) checked. Fadus’s 44–46 CE administration is later than Judas’s census revolt. A different earlier Theudas is possible but not established by these sources. |

The new records quote the packaged KJV, checked by exact string comparison. Existing Greek/Hebrew findings and summaries were not rewritten or repurposed as historical proof. No T, V or Q findings were invented for this audit. In particular, the new Jericho flag concerns an archaeological layer, separate from the existing textual conquest-summary and Rahab questions; the new Darius flag concerns royal succession, separate from the existing Daniel 11:1 Greek/Cyrus variant; and the Theudas flag concerns sequence, separate from the existing Judas movement corroboration.

The comparison heading is **Academic evidence**, because these conditional arguments and individual studies do not justify claiming uniform academic consensus. Drawers include the relevant summary, scope limits, source links and review date. No-flag cards show their limited review outcome.
