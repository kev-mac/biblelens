**TL;DR:** The comparison boards are ready. They bring together the independently discovered controls, cards and panels—not just your examples—so you can choose useful elements from either generation. Keep one, several or all styles per group and say where each should be used. No choices are assumed and no app files changed.

**[Open the complete comparison worksheet](comparison-boards.html)** — 16 groups, 144 source-component families and 26 image sheets. Each group includes an expandable gallery of every captured appearance, its location, dimensions, colors, selectors and source lines. Families are not a claim that every family has a unique visual skin.

**How to answer:** `D4 keep A,C; A for search boxes, C’s magnifier on all searches. D2 keep B,I for direct passages and C,F,H for citations.` Letter IDs belong to their group. You may select individual appearances in the HTML gallery if you want a particular theme/state example, rather than the whole family. Nothing in the worksheet changes the app. Download selections before closing it, or reply in chat.

**Benefits and tradeoffs:** One style within a matching job is easiest to recognize but may remove useful page cues. Several styles with clear usage rules preserve useful differences but need maintenance. Keeping every current style changes no controls, but leaves the documented usability issues. All these style choices are reversible; a 44px touch-target/focus pass can be chosen independently. Any app-wide P2 redesign remains outside this review.

**Scope:** The original control matrix covers dark/light and desktop/phone at 100% text. New rich-object captures retain your 115% setting and are labeled separately. Source-only, account-only, native-only and unforced-error states are identified in [coverage-gaps.md](coverage-gaps.md); they are not passed off as live screenshots.
