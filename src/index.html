About

Fair examination means tracing evidence, identifying assumptions, and leaving uncertainty visible.

Attribution

Reading views show our explanatory summaries and assessments, based on the passages and available evidence. SAB wording is not reproduced; background references remain available in Sources. Our interpretations are provisional and can be mistaken.

This is an independent concept, not an official SAB product. Original annotations are copyrighted by their author; the full archive has not been copied.

Evidence

Categories show verse-aligned historical, textual and scientific assessments. Context marks observations that need a closer reading of the setting and wording. History and Science use sourced editorial categories; empty cells mark gaps in this collection.

A reconciliation can be possible without being historically demonstrated. Textual observations, theological premises, and independent corroboration have different roles. We do not use numerical credibility scores or give all arguments equal evidentiary weight.

Categories

Questions is a separate category alongside Science, History and Text Comparisons, marking a significant gap in the passage’s explanation. It marks a question that needs more context. Click a Q tag in Read to show its summary; details keep the reasoning, sources and interpretation limits.

These are provisional editorial classifications, based on the text and interpretation involved. Counts show the number of records in each collection.

Features

No public comments, voting, leaderboards, embedded ads, or required research accounts. No automatically published AI annotations, AI text comparison ratings, or chatbot presented as an authority.

The evidence finder and Chat search the packaged Bible text and records. Chat presents stored passages and editorial summaries conversationally, with linked sources. It uses no AI service or live web search and requires no separate login. Missing matches are acknowledged. The History and Science categories are provisional editorial analyses of linked sources, with limits shown alongside agreement, disagreement or context labels.

Sources

For English reading of Septuagint categories, use NETS ? New English Translation of the Septuagint. NETS is linked from its official electronic edition; its copyrighted files are not redistributed here. Existing records retain their original quotation-source citations pending edition-specific review.

The reader includes all 66 books and 1,189 chapters in World English Bible (WEB), KJV, and American Standard Version (1901). WEB is packaged from the public-domain eBible.org Protestant Edition; KJV and ASV come from the Midvash public-domain dataset. Missing numbered verses in an edition are shown as absent in that dataset, never silently filled from another translation. KJV/ASV provenance ↗ WEB source and downloads ↗

Red letters follow eBible.org words-of-Jesus markup in KJV and WEB. ASV uses aligned KJV speech boundaries, with differing speaker transitions checked individually. Red-letter boundaries are editorial; some endpoints, including John 3, are debated.

SAB and the two GotQuestions response pages were checked October 11, 2026. GotQuestions is a confessional Christian source, not a substitute for peer-reviewed historical scholarship.

Privacy

Bookmarks and notes stay on this device unless you enable Cloud sync in Settings. Optional sync uses your ChatGPT sign-in and private cloud storage to keep notes, bookmarks, and reader preferences together across connected devices. JSON and Markdown exports remain available. A shared passage link shares the chapter or record, not your notes. Chat searches the included Bible text, reference aids, and research records on this site. Questions and recent conversation context are sent only to this site’s search endpoint, with no external AI service. Chat history lasts for this page session; private notes are not sent.

Hosted access follows the site’s sharing settings. Chat requires no separate login.
